# Enzymes: exam-style question (AQA GCSE Combined Science: Biology, Foundation Tier)

**Total: 8 marks** | Topic: Enzymes (active site, temperature, pH, denaturing) | Suggested time: about 10 minutes

**Assumptions:** AQA Combined Science Trilogy, Foundation Tier, Paper 1 style (Topic 4.2, Bioenergetics and Topic 4.1 enzyme content in the specification). Original question, not taken from a past paper. All four parts share one context, so the "describe" part leads into the "explain" parts.

---

## Question

A student investigated the enzyme **amylase**. Amylase is found in saliva. It breaks down **starch**.

The student mixed amylase and starch at six different temperatures. She measured how fast the starch was broken down. Her results are shown in the table.

| Temperature in °C | Rate of reaction in arbitrary units |
|:---:|:---:|
| 10 | 2 |
| 20 | 5 |
| 30 | 9 |
| 40 | 14 |
| 50 | 6 |
| 60 | 0 |

**(a)** Give **one** substance made when amylase breaks down starch. **[1]**

**(b)** **Describe** how the rate of reaction changes as the temperature increases from 10 °C to 60 °C. **[2]**

**(c)** At 60 °C the rate of reaction was zero.
**Explain** why the rate of reaction was zero at 60 °C. **[3]**

**(d)** Amylase works best at pH 7. The stomach contains acid and has a pH of about 2.
**Explain** why amylase does **not** work in the stomach. **[2]**

**Total for question: 8 marks**

---

## Mark scheme

### (a) [1 mark]

| Answer | Mark |
|---|:---:|
| Sugar(s) **or** glucose **or** maltose | 1 |

**Accept:** simple sugars, reducing sugars, sugar molecules.
**Do not accept:** "energy" (not a substance made in this reaction); "amino acids" (that is protein breakdown); "fat" or "starch".

### (b) [2 marks]

| Marking point | Mark |
|---|:---:|
| Rate increases (as temperature rises) from 10 °C to 40 °C **(1)** | 1 |
| Rate then decreases from 40 °C to 60 °C (and reaches zero at 60 °C) **(1)** | 1 |

**Accept:** "goes up then goes down"; "highest rate at 40 °C"; use of figures from the table, for example "rate rises from 2 to 14 then falls to 0".
**Do not accept:** "rate increases then stays the same" (not shown by the data); "rate goes up" with no mention of the fall (only 1 mark).

*Note for markers: (b) is a "describe" question. Do not credit reasons here. Reasons earn marks in (c).*

### (c) [3 marks]

| Marking point | Mark |
|---|:---:|
| The high temperature changes the shape of the **active site** (of the enzyme) **(1)** | 1 |
| So the substrate / starch no longer fits (into the active site) **(1)** | 1 |
| So the starch is not broken down / the reaction cannot happen / no product is made **(1)** | 1 |

**Accept:**
- "Enzyme is denatured" for the first mark. The shape change must be linked to the enzyme or active site.
- "Enzyme changes shape" for the first mark.
- "Starch cannot fit / bind / attach to the enzyme" for the second mark.

**Do not accept:**
- "The enzyme is killed" or "dies" (enzymes are not alive).
- "The enzyme melts" or "burns".
- "The enzyme stops working" on its own. This restates the question and gives no reason.
- "The starch changes shape" (it is the enzyme that changes).

*Note for markers: the second and third marks depend on a sensible link ("so", "because", "therefore"). Credit the marking points in any order.*

### (d) [2 marks]

| Marking point | Mark |
|---|:---:|
| The acid / low pH changes the shape of the active site (of amylase) **(1)** | 1 |
| So the starch no longer fits (into the active site) / amylase is denatured and cannot break down starch **(1)** | 1 |

**Accept:** "pH 2 is too far from the best pH for amylase, so the enzyme is denatured" for 1 mark, then a link to starch not fitting or no reaction for the second mark.
**Do not accept:** "The acid kills the enzyme"; "acid breaks down the starch" (not the point being tested); "amylase only works in the mouth" with no reason.

---

## Model answers (full marks)

**(a)** Glucose. **[1]**

**(b)** The rate of reaction **increases** from 2 units at 10 °C to a maximum of 14 units at 40 °C. It then **decreases** and falls to 0 at 60 °C. **[2]**

**(c)** At 60 °C the high temperature **changes the shape of the active site**, so the enzyme is **denatured**. **Because** the shape has changed, the starch **can no longer fit** into the active site. **Therefore** the starch is not broken down and the rate of reaction is zero. **[3]**

**(d)** The acid in the stomach **changes the shape of the active site** of amylase, so amylase is denatured. **This means** the starch can no longer fit into the active site, so amylase cannot break the starch down. **[2]**

## Why this question helps with "explain"

Your students struggle with explain questions, so the parts are built as a ladder.

| Part | Command word | What the student does |
|---|---|---|
| (a) | Give | Recall one fact |
| (b) | Describe | Say **what** happens (no reasons) |
| (c) | Explain | Say what happens and **why**, in a chain |
| (d) | Explain | Repeat the same chain in a new situation (pH instead of temperature) |

The "explain" answer is a three-link chain. Teach it as a sentence stem:

> **Cause** (high temperature / acid) **changes** the shape of the active site, **so** the substrate no longer fits, **so** no reaction happens.

Each link is a mark. Students who only write "the enzyme stops working" get 0 for (c), because that is the outcome, not the reason.

**Classroom use:**
1. Have students answer (b) and (c) side by side. Ask them to underline the **linking words** (because, so, therefore, this means) in the model answer.
2. Give them the sentence stem above and let them answer (d) with it.
3. Peer-mark using the marking points, one tick per link.
4. Extension for stronger students: swap pH 2 for a new context (for example, a protease in the small intestine) and ask them to write the chain with no stem.

---

## Quality check

- Command words match marks: Give (1), Describe (2), Explain (3), Explain (2).
- Each mark can be earned independently, and each "explain" mark has a different marking point.
- Content stays inside the foundation specification: active site, specific shape, temperature, pH, denaturing. No "complementary" or "optimum" terminology is required to gain marks.
- Difficulty rises from (a) to (c), then (d) tests transfer to a new variable.
- No multiple-choice items, so no answer patterns to check.
