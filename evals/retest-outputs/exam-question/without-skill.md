# Enzymes: exam-style question (AQA GCSE Combined Science, Foundation Tier)

**Total: 8 marks | Suggested time: about 9 minutes**

*Assumptions: this is written in the style of AQA Trilogy/Synergy Paper 1 (Biology), at Foundation Tier. It uses amylase and pH because this is a very common required-practical context. The 4-mark "explain" part is the one to focus on with your class.*

---

## Question

A student investigated the effect of pH on the enzyme amylase.

**Method**

1. She put a drop of iodine solution into each well of a spotting tile.
2. She mixed amylase solution, starch solution and a buffer solution at one pH in a test tube.
3. Every 30 seconds she used a dropper to take a sample from the test tube and add it to a new drop of iodine solution.
4. She stopped when the iodine solution stayed orange-brown.
5. She repeated steps 1 to 4 with buffer solutions at different pH values. All the tubes were kept at 30 °C.

**Results**

| pH of buffer | Time taken for starch to be digested in minutes |
|:---:|:---:|
| 3 | Not digested |
| 5 | 6.0 |
| 7 | 1.5 |
| 9 | 5.5 |
| 11 | Not digested |

---

**0 1 . 1** Name the substrate of amylase. **[1 mark]**

**0 1 . 2** What is the optimum pH for amylase, using the results in the table? **[1 mark]**

**0 1 . 3** Iodine solution is orange-brown.
Describe what the student would see when iodine solution is added to a sample that still contains starch. **[1 mark]**

**0 1 . 4** At pH 11 the starch was **not** digested.
Explain why. **[4 marks]**

*Your answer should include what happens to the enzyme and why the substrate cannot be broken down.*

**0 1 . 5** Suggest **one** variable the student controlled, other than temperature, to make this a fair test. **[1 mark]**

**Total for Question 1: 8 marks**

## Model answer for 01.4 (4 marks)

> At pH 11 the conditions are too alkaline, so the amylase is **denatured**. This changes the **shape of the active site**. The starch **no longer fits** into the active site, so an **enzyme-substrate complex cannot form** and the starch is not broken down.

## Notes for teaching "explain" questions

Students at Foundation Tier often lose marks on "explain" because they describe the result ("the enzyme stopped working") without giving the reason. A simple scaffold that works well for enzyme questions is a chain of linked statements:

**Point, because, so.**

| Step | Sentence starter | Example from this question |
|---|---|---|
| **Point** (what happened) | "The starch was not digested..." | The starch was not digested at pH 11 |
| **Because** (the cause) | "...because the enzyme was..." | because the enzyme was denatured |
| **So** (the consequence) | "...so the substrate could not..." | so the starch could not fit the active site |

**Common errors seen in student answers (worth showing on the board):**

- "The enzyme is killed / dies." Enzymes are proteins, not living things. Use **denatured**.
- "The enzyme is denatured" with no mention of the **active site** or **shape**. This gets 1 mark at most.
- "The starch is denatured." Only the enzyme can be denatured.
- Saying the enzyme "melts" or "burns" (this confuses temperature and pH effects).
- Repeating the question ("it did not work because it was too alkaline") without saying what happens to the enzyme.

**Quick check before students write:** ask them to underline the command word (*Explain*), then ask "Have I said **what happens to the enzyme**, **what happens to the active site**, and **what that means for the substrate**?" Three ideas usually gives you 3 to 4 marks.

**Extension (Higher-style stretch, optional):** "Explain why the enzyme also works slowly at pH 5, but does not stop completely." (Answer idea: the pH is close to the optimum / the active site is only slightly changed, so some substrate still fits.)
