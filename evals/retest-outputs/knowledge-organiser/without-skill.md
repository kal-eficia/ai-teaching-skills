# Knowledge Organiser: The Norman Conquest, 1066

**Year 7 History | Big question: How did William of Normandy win England and keep it?**

*Assumptions: about 6 lessons, mixed-ability class, designed to fit on one double-sided A4 sheet. Dates and figures are the commonly taught ones; adjust to match your textbook.*

## 2. The four claimants to the throne

| Claimant | Who he was | His claim | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Harold Godwinson** | Earl of Wessex, the most powerful noble in England; Edward's brother-in-law | Said Edward named him on his deathbed; chosen by the **Witan** (council of nobles) | Already in England; had an army; the Witan backed him | Not of royal blood; had sworn an oath to William (1064) |
| **William, Duke of Normandy** | Edward's distant cousin; a strong, experienced military leader | Said Edward promised him the throne years earlier, and that Harold swore to support him | Skilled commander; Pope's blessing; Norman knights | Had to cross the Channel; no English support |
| **Harald Hardrada** | King of Norway; famous Viking warrior | Based on an old agreement between earlier Norwegian and English kings | Fierce army; allied with Harold's exiled brother Tostig | Far from home; little support in England |
| **Edgar the Ætheling** | Teenage great-nephew of Edward; the only one of royal blood | Blood relative of the old king | Royal blood | Too young; no army; no powerful backers |

## 3. Key events

### Stamford Bridge (25 September 1066)
- Hardrada and Tostig invaded the north, won at Fulford, and camped near York.
- Harold marched his army north in around four days (roughly 180 miles) and took them by surprise.
- The English won a huge victory. **Hardrada and Tostig were both killed.** Only a tiny fraction of the Viking fleet sailed home.
- **Why it matters:** it removed one rival, but Harold's army was left tired and weakened. Three days later William landed in the south.

### Hastings (14 October 1066)
- Harold rushed south (about 250 miles) and took a position on **Senlac Hill**.
- The English fought in a **shield wall**, mostly on foot with axes and spears.
- The Normans had **cavalry, archers and infantry**. Their attacks could not break the wall at first.
- Some Normans pretended to retreat (a **feigned retreat**). English soldiers chased them downhill and the wall broke up.
- Harold was killed (the Bayeux Tapestry is often read as showing an arrow in the eye, but historians disagree).
- **Why William won:** better mix of troops, clever tactics, Harold's army tired from the march, Harold's decision to fight quickly rather than wait for more men, and some luck.

## 4. How William kept control

### Castles
- Built fast across the country to guard towns, roads and rivers.
- First **motte and bailey** castles: a mound (**motte**) with a wooden tower on top and an enclosed yard (**bailey**), surrounded by a ditch and fence. These could go up in days.
- Later rebuilt in stone, for example the **Tower of London (White Tower)**.
- **Purpose:** a safe base for soldiers, a symbol of Norman power, and a way to scare and control local people.

### The feudal system
A system where land was swapped for loyalty and service.

```
KING (William owns all the land)
   |  gives land in return for loyalty and soldiers
BARONS (tenants-in-chief)
   |  receive land, provide knights
KNIGHTS
   |  receive land, fight for the baron
PEASANTS / VILLEINS
      work the land, pay rent in labour or food; not free to leave
```

- William kept about a quarter of the land for himself, gave much to the Church, and gave the rest to loyal Normans.
- Barons gave **homage** (a promise of loyalty). At **Salisbury in 1086** all major landowners swore their oath directly to the King.

### The Domesday Book (1085-1086)
- William sent officials across England to record who owned what land, how many people, animals and ploughs there were, and what it was worth.
- **Purpose:** to find out the wealth of the country, so William could collect the right taxes and know who held what.
- The name comes from "Doomsday", the Day of Judgement: like God's judgement, there was no appeal against what it recorded.
- It is still one of the most useful sources historians have about life in medieval England.

### Other ways William used force
- **Harrying of the North (1069-1070):** after northern rebellions, William's army burned villages and crops. Thousands starved. A brutal warning to others.
- **Hereward the Wake** led resistance from the Isle of Ely (1070-71) but was defeated.
- English lords lost their land to Normans; Norman French became the language of the ruling class.

## 5. Key vocabulary

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Claimant** | Someone who says they have the right to be king |
| **Witan** | The council of English nobles that advised the king and helped choose him |
| **Earl** | A powerful English noble who ruled a large area |
| **Duke** | A powerful noble in Normandy (William's title) |
| **Shield wall** | Soldiers locked their shields together in a tight line |
| **Cavalry** | Soldiers who fight on horseback |
| **Feigned retreat** | A fake retreat to trick the enemy |
| **Motte and bailey** | The first Norman castle design: a mound and an enclosed yard |
| **Feudal system** | A system where land is exchanged for loyalty and service |
| **Baron / tenant-in-chief** | A noble who held land directly from the king |
| **Knight** | A trained mounted soldier who held land from a baron |
| **Villein** | A peasant tied to the lord's land |
| **Homage** | A promise of loyalty to a lord or king |
| **Domesday Book** | William's survey of all land and property in England |
| **Harrying** | Raiding and destroying a place |
| **Bayeux Tapestry** | A long embroidered cloth telling the story of 1066 from the Norman point of view |
| **Source** | Evidence from the past, such as a tapestry, chronicle or book |

## 7. Think like a historian

- **Cause:** Why did Edward's death lead to war? (No clear heir, and four people with claims.)
- **Chance or skill?** Was Hastings won because of William's skill, or because of Harold's bad luck (two battles in three weeks)? Give evidence on both sides.
- **Source question:** The Bayeux Tapestry was made on Norman orders. How might that affect what it shows?
- **Significance:** What were the *most* important ways William kept control: castles, the feudal system, or the Domesday Book? Why?
