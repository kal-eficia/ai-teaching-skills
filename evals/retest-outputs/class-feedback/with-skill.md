## Student sheet: Mr Birling essays

### What went well
- **You know the play.** Almost everyone showed secure knowledge of the plot and of what Birling does and says.
- **Strong links to capitalism.** Several of you saw that Birling stands for a whole way of thinking about money and society, and not only for one man. That is the kind of idea that gets top marks.

### Your code
Look at the letter on your essay. Find it below and do that task.

**A. Analyse, don't retell**
*The problem:* Your paragraph tells me what happens. It doesn't say what Priestley is doing or why.
*The fix:* After each plot point, answer: "What does this make the audience think about Birling, and why did Priestley want that?" Replace "Then Birling says..." with "Priestley presents Birling as... because..."

**B. Comment on the language**
*The problem:* You included a quote but then moved on without saying anything about it.
*The fix:* Pick one word or phrase from the quote. Name its effect (for example: confident, dismissive, selfish, foolish). Say what that tells us about Birling. A quote with no comment on the words is only half a point.

**C. Add context (1912 and 1945)**
*The problem:* Your essay doesn't mention when the play is set and when it was written.
*The fix:* Add one sentence that links the two dates to Birling. The play is set in 1912, before the First World War and before the Titanic sank. It was written in 1945, after two world wars. So the audience already knows Birling's confident predictions are wrong. Say what that makes us think of him and of the people he represents.

**D. Shorter, clearer paragraphs**
*The problem:* Your paragraph tries to do too much, so the point gets lost.
*The fix:* One paragraph, one idea. Start with a clear point, then evidence, then analysis. If you find yourself starting a second idea, start a new paragraph.

*Everyone also checks the spelling list below.*

### Model paragraph: what earns the marks
Notice the three parts: **point, evidence with a comment on the words, context.**

> Priestley presents Mr Birling as a self-satisfied capitalist who puts profit above people. Early in the play, Birling says a man has to "look after himself and his own". The word "own" shows how small his circle of concern is: he only cares about his family and his business, and the verb "look after" makes this sound like a sensible duty, which shows how he excuses his selfishness. This was written in 1945, after two world wars had shown how much people depended on each other, so Priestley's audience would see this attitude as outdated and dangerous. Birling is therefore not just one man, but a warning about a whole way of thinking.

*What to copy:* it begins with a clear point about Birling. The quote is short and fits into the sentence. The comment picks out particular words. The context is used to explain why Priestley wrote it, not just dropped in as a fact.

### Spelling and key words
| Word | Common mistake | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| **Priestley** | Priestly | It starts with **Priest**, then **ley**. Say it slowly: Priest-ley. |
| **capitalism** | capatalism, capitolism | It comes from the word **capital** (money), with **ism** on the end. |
| **responsibility** | responsability | **Responsible** ends in **ible**, so the noun is **responsibility** with an **i**. |

## For you (teacher only)

**How to code the essays (about 5 minutes in total)**
- Write the single most important letter on each essay. Based on your notes, the likely spread is: many students with **A**, about half with **B**, and nearly everyone with **C**. Give students who need more than one a second letter.
- Students who already did well on all three and linked Birling to capitalism can skip the codes. Give them the stretch: *"Explain how Priestley uses Birling to criticise capitalism, using a quote and the 1945 context together."*

**Reteach to the whole class (suggested order)**
1. **Context as explanation**, not as a fact tacked on. Almost nobody used it, so a 10-minute mini-lesson on 1912 versus 1945 and how it changes how the audience sees Birling will have the biggest effect.
2. **Quote, then zoom into a word.** Model it live with one Birling line.
3. **Analysis versus retelling.** Use the sentence starter "Priestley presents Birling as... because..."
4. **Paragraph structure** (point, evidence, analysis, context link), with a limit of about one idea each.

**Before you print:** if you can, check the wording of the "look after himself and his own" quotation against your class copy of the play, and swap in a quote your students have already studied if you prefer.
