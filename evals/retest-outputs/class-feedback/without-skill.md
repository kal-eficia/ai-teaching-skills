# Whole-class feedback: Priestley and Mr Birling

You don't need to write on 28 essays. Your notes already hold what a whole-class feedback sheet needs. Give the class one sheet, a short lesson to go with it, and a few quick individual marks. Here is a plan you can use as it stands.

*Assumptions: the essays were a timed or standard GCSE-style response, you have about 60 to 75 minutes of lesson time available, and you want pupils to redraft one paragraph rather than the whole essay.*

---

## 1. The one-page sheet to hand back

### What you did well
- Your knowledge of the plot is secure. You know what Birling says and does, and when.
- Several of you linked Birling to capitalism. That is exactly the kind of idea that earns top marks. Keep it.
- Many of you backed up points with quotations.

### Your three targets
Each target has a "so what" fix, because most of the problems come from the same habit: describing instead of explaining.

**Target 1: Analyse, don't retell**
- Retelling: *Birling says the Titanic is unsinkable and then the Inspector arrives.*
- Analysing: *Priestley makes Birling confidently proclaim the Titanic "unsinkable", so the audience, who know it sank, see that his judgement is not to be trusted.*
- Test: after each sentence, ask "So what? What is Priestley doing here?" If you can't answer, you are retelling.

**Target 2: Zoom in on the language**
- A quotation is evidence, not the point. After every quote, comment on one word or technique.
- Try: **Quote, zoom, effect.**
  - *Quote:* Birling dismisses war as "fiddlesticks".
  - *Zoom:* The word "fiddlesticks" is dismissive and trivial.
  - *Effect:* It shows he is arrogant and out of touch, so the audience trust him less.

**Target 3: Bring in context**
- The play is set in **1912** but was written in **1945**.
- Birling is wrong about war, the Titanic and workers' rights. The 1945 audience had lived through two world wars, so they see his mistakes immediately. Priestley uses him to show the failure of the old, selfish capitalist attitudes.
- Weave context into your analysis. Don't add it as a separate paragraph.
- Try: *"Priestley, writing in 1945, presents Birling as... because..."*

### Quick fixes
- **Spellings to learn this week:** Priestley, capitalism, responsibility
  - Priestley: *Priest-ley*, as in a priest.
  - Capitalism: *capital* + *ism*.
  - Responsibility: *respons-ibility*, as in "able to respond".
- **Paragraphs:** Aim for one idea per paragraph. If a paragraph runs past about 8 to 10 lines, find where the idea changes and start a new one.

---

## 2. Model paragraph for the board

Show this and annotate it with the class (retell, quote, zoom and context are marked by the labels).

> Priestley presents Mr Birling as arrogant and short-sighted. He declares that the Titanic is "unsinkable" **[quote]**, and the absolute adjective suggests he cannot imagine being wrong **[zoom]**. Writing in 1945, Priestley knew his audience would recognise the irony, as the ship sank in 1912 **[context]**. This makes Birling a figure of dramatic irony, so the audience distrust his capitalist views and are encouraged to favour collective responsibility **[effect and link to Priestley's message]**.

Ask pupils to find: the quote, the language comment, the context, and the "so what".

---

## 3. A 60-minute lesson to go with it

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 5 min | Hand back essays and the sheet. Pupils read it and circle which target applies most to them. |
| 10 min | Show the retell vs analyse examples. Pupils sort five sentences into "retelling" or "analysing" and fix the retelling ones. |
| 10 min | Model paragraph on the board. Colour-code quote, zoom, context and effect. |
| 5 min | Spelling quick-fire: pupils write the three words from memory, check, and correct. |
| 25 min | **Redraft one paragraph** from their essay, using their chosen target. |
| 5 min | Swap with a partner. The partner ticks: *quote? language comment? context? one idea?* |

## 5. Quick individual feedback (about 10 to 15 minutes in total)

You do not need comments on all 28. Do this instead:

- **The strong capitalism essays:** write one line, "Develop this idea further and add context", and ask them to read their paragraph aloud as a model.
- **The weakest retellings:** write one line, "Try one analytical sentence per paragraph", or give them a sentence starter.
- **Everyone else:** circle the target (1, 2 or 3) that matters most to them and nothing else.

---

## 6. Next steps for you

- Check next week's paragraphs, not whole essays, for evidence the targets have landed.
- If context is the weakest area (almost nobody used it), plan a short follow-up lesson on 1912 vs 1945 before the next essay.
- Keep two or three of the strong capitalism paragraphs (anonymised) to show as examples next time.
