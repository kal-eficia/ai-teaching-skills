### 2. The four claimants

2.1 Edward the Confessor died childless in January 1066, so nobody had a clear right to the throne.
2.2 **Harold Godwinson** was the most powerful earl in England. The Witan chose him and he was crowned the next day.
2.3 **William, Duke of Normandy** said Edward had promised him the throne and that Harold had sworn an oath in 1064 to support him.
2.4 **Harald Hardrada**, King of Norway, based his claim on an old agreement between earlier Norwegian and English kings.
2.5 **Edgar Aetheling** was Edward's great-nephew and had royal blood, but he was only about 14 and had little support.

### 3. Two battles in three weeks

3.1 Harold marched his army about 200 miles north in days to surprise Hardrada and Tostig, and beat them at **Stamford Bridge** (25 Sep).
3.2 Hardrada was killed. Harold's army was tired and had lost men.
3.3 Three days later William's army landed at Pevensey while the wind had kept his fleet in port. Harold had to march south again.
3.4 At **Hastings** (14 Oct) the English fought on a hill in a **shield wall**.
3.5 William's army had cavalry and archers as well as foot soldiers. The English had almost no cavalry or archers.
3.6 Some Normans pretended to run away (a **feigned retreat**). English soldiers chased them and broke the shield wall.
3.7 Harold was killed and the English lost. William was crowned on Christmas Day 1066.

### 4. Castles

4.1 William built castles at key towns and roads to control the English and protect his soldiers.
4.2 The first castles were **motte and bailey**, built from earth and timber in days or weeks.
4.3 Later many were rebuilt in stone, so they lasted and were harder to attack.
4.4 Castles were a base for soldiers, a home for the lord, and a sign of Norman power.
4.5 When the north rebelled in 1069, William crushed it (the "Harrying of the North"), burning farms and crops.

### 5. The feudal system

5.1 William said that all land in England belonged to the king.
5.2 He gave land to a few trusted Normans in return for loyalty and soldiers.
5.3 Each level held land from the level above and owed it something in return.
5.4 Peasants (villeins) worked the land for their lord and owed him work. They had no say.
5.5 Everyone swore an oath of loyalty to William, so he could rely on thousands of armed men.

### 6. Domesday Book

6.1 William ordered a great survey of England at Christmas 1085. It was finished in 1086.
6.2 Officials asked in every village who owned the land, how much there was, and what it was worth.
6.3 It let William see what he had conquered and how much tax he could collect.
6.4 People later nicknamed it "Domesday" because its decisions were final, like Judgement Day. There was no appeal.

### 7. Key vocabulary

| # | Word | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| 7.1 | claimant | A person who says they have the right to something, here the throne |
| 7.2 | heir | Someone who inherits a title or property when the owner dies |
| 7.3 | earl | A powerful English noble who governed a large area for the king |
| 7.4 | Witan | The council of leading English nobles and church leaders who advised the king |
| 7.5 | shield wall | A tight line of soldiers with overlapping shields |
| 7.6 | cavalry | Soldiers who fight on horseback |
| 7.7 | feigned retreat | Pretending to run away to trick the enemy into chasing you |
| 7.8 | motte | A raised mound of earth with a wooden tower on top |
| 7.9 | bailey | The enclosed yard at the foot of the motte, inside a fence |
| 7.10 | feudal system | A way of organising society where land is held in return for loyalty and service |
| 7.11 | baron | A powerful lord who held land directly from the king |
| 7.12 | knight | A mounted soldier who held land from a baron in return for fighting for him |
| 7.13 | villein | A peasant who worked his lord's land and was tied to it |
| 7.14 | Domesday Book | William's record of who owned land in England and what it was worth |

### 9. Common mistakes (not this, but this)

9.1 **Not:** "Harold fought at Hastings straight after a rest." **But:** he had just marched about 200 miles from the north, after Stamford Bridge.
9.2 **Not:** "William fought Harold Hardrada." **But:** Harold Godwinson fought Hardrada at Stamford Bridge, and William at Hastings. Three men, two Harolds.
9.3 **Not:** "William killed all the English nobles and kept the old system." **But:** he gave most of the land to Normans, using the feudal system, and the English nobles lost power.
9.4 **Not:** "The Domesday Book is a list of castles." **But:** it records who owned land and what it was worth, so William could tax it.
9.5 **Not:** "All castles were stone fortresses." **But:** the first were quick earth and timber motte and baileys, and stone came later.

**Leads to:** Norman life and the medieval Church, the Magna Carta (1215), and how kings and barons shared power later.
