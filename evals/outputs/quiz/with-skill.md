# Year 7 Geography: Rivers Low-Stakes Check

**Assumptions (brief):** The unit finished two weeks ago, so every question is retrieval from the rivers unit. Two questions (3 and 10) draw on the earlier map skills work, set in a river context. Topics are mixed so neighbouring questions never share a topic. Takes about 8 minutes. I haven't seen your teaching materials, so please check the wording of terms (for example "abrasion", "suspension") matches what you taught.

## Teacher answer key and misconception guide

| Q | Topic | Answer | Where taught |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Source to mouth | B | Rivers unit, course of a river |
| 2 | Erosion | D | Rivers unit, erosion processes |
| 3 | Map skills (contours) | A | Map skills unit, relief and contours |
| 4 | Transportation | C | Rivers unit, transportation |
| 5 | Meanders | A | Rivers unit, landforms |
| 6 | Deposition | D | Rivers unit, deposition |
| 7 | Waterfalls | B | Rivers unit, landforms |
| 8 | Source to mouth | C | Rivers unit, course of a river |
| 9 | Flooding | A | Rivers unit, flooding |
| 10 | Map skills (compass directions) | B | Map skills unit, direction |
| 11 | Oxbow lakes (meanders and deposition) | D | Rivers unit, landforms |
| 12 | Upper course valley shape | C | Rivers unit, course of a river and erosion |

**What each wrong option reveals**

1. A: confuses source with mouth. C: confuses source with confluence. D: guesses from the word "source" without knowing the course.
2. A: mixes up abrasion (rocks scraping the bed and banks) with attrition (rocks hitting each other and getting smaller and rounder). B: mixes it up with hydraulic action (force of water). C: mixes it up with solution (dissolving rock such as limestone).
3. B: thinks close lines mean flat land, or reads contours backwards. C: confuses contours with vegetation symbols. D: thinks contours show depth below sea level.
4. A: confuses it with traction (large boulders rolled along the bed). B: confuses it with saltation (small stones bouncing along). D: confuses it with solution (minerals dissolved, invisible in the water).
5. B: believes slow water does the eroding (confuses the inside, where deposition happens, with the outside). C: thinks a bend is symmetrical. D: thinks erosion only happens in straight sections.
6. A and B: think more energy means dropping the load. C: thinks more water means deposition (it usually gives more energy, so more erosion and transport).
7. A: confuses waterfalls with deposition features. C: confuses them with flood processes. D: confuses them with a confluence.
8. A: confuses mouth with source. B: confuses mouth with confluence. D: guesses from the everyday meaning of "mouth" with no sense of the course.
9. B: thinks dry soil absorbs nothing and light rain floods. C: thinks trees increase flooding (they intercept rain and slow it). D: thinks a bigger channel increases flood risk.
10. A: reads direction as where the river comes from. C and D: don't confuse the compass points properly.
11. A: confuses it with waterfall retreat. B: confuses it with floodplain formation. C: confuses it with glacial landforms.
12. A: mixes the upper course with the lower course (wide, flat floodplain). B: mixes it up with the middle and lower course (meanders). D: mixes it up with deposition, which is mostly in the lower course.

**Answer-pattern check (done by hand and by script):** The key is B D A C A D B C A B D C. Checked with the no-answer-patterns script: clean. By hand: no two neighbours match, no three in a row run up or down (such as A B C or D C B), no two letters alternating four times, no block of three to five letters repeats, and each letter is correct exactly 3 times out of 12.

**What to do with the results:** Students answer from memory, then check and correct in a different colour. Note the two most-missed questions and use them as starters next lesson. Look at the confidence column for "certain" answers that were wrong, as these are the most useful misconceptions to address.
