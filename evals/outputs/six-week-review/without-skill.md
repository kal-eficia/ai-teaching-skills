# Six-Week Review: [S1], Year 11 GCSE Maths

**Short answer for the parents: yes, the tutoring is working, and the progress is real. One topic, ratio and proportion, needs a different approach, and that is where the next phase of work will focus.**

*Assumptions: the week 1 and week 6 tests were the same style and difficulty, and scores are percentages on each topic section. Statistics was not tested in week 1, so it is left out of the before-and-after comparison.*

---

## Results at a glance

| Topic | Week 1 | Week 6 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Algebra | 35% | 70% | +35 points |
| Number | 60% | 75% | +15 points |
| Ratio and proportion | 40% | 45% | +5 points |
| Geometry | 30% | 55% | +25 points |
| Probability | 50% | 50% | no change |
| Statistics | not tested | 65% | new baseline |

**Overall:** across the five topics tested both times, the average rose from **43% to 59%**, a gain of 16 percentage points in six weeks.

## What has gone well

- **Algebra has doubled (35% to 70%).** This is the biggest result, and it matters most: algebra carries the largest share of marks on the GCSE paper and feeds into ratio, geometry and graphs.
- **Geometry is up 25 points (30% to 55%).** It started as the weakest topic and is now moving in the right direction.
- **Number has gone from 60% to 75%.** This is now the strongest area, which gives a solid base to work from.
- **Statistics came in at 65%** on its first test, which is a good starting point.
- **Attendance is excellent: 11 of 12 sessions (92%).** Regular attendance is almost certainly a big part of why the scores moved.

## What needs attention

- **Ratio and proportion barely moved (40% to 45%).** [S1] describes ratio as "confusing" and tends to avoid it. Avoidance is the key issue here. It is hard to improve at something you steer away from, so the flat result is unsurprising. Ratio is also worth a meaningful share of GCSE marks and shows up inside other topics (scale, speed, percentages, problem solving), so leaving it weak will cost marks across the paper.
- **Probability is unchanged at 50%.** It has had less attention while the larger gains were chased, so this is a gap in coverage rather than a sign of difficulty.
- **Homework: 4 of 6 completed (67%).** Reasonable, but this is the easiest lever to pull. The topics where [S1] did the most work are the ones that improved most, and ratio is the topic where independent practice would help most.

## So is the tutoring working?

Yes. A 16-point average gain in six weeks, with two topics improving by 25 to 35 points, is a strong result for a Year 11 student, and attendance has been very good.

Two honest caveats for the parents:

1. **This is one test at each end.** Topic scores come from a limited number of questions, so a few points either way (such as ratio's +5) are within normal variation. The large gains in algebra and geometry are too big to be put down to chance, but I would not read too much into small differences.
2. **The gains are not yet spread evenly.** Overall performance on the real paper will depend on the weakest areas as much as the strongest, so the focus now shifts to ratio, probability and geometry.

## Plan for the next six weeks

1. **Rebuild ratio from the ground up.** Go back to the basics (simplifying ratios, sharing in a ratio, then proportion and recipes/scale problems) using short, low-pressure questions so that [S1] gets quick wins and stops avoiding it. Use bar models and double number lines to make it less "confusing". Ratio will open every session for a few weeks, regardless of the main topic.
2. **Keep the algebra momentum.** Move on to harder algebra (quadratics, simultaneous equations, rearranging formulae), since this is where the most marks sit.
3. **Push geometry from 55% towards 70%+** (angles, area and volume, Pythagoras, trigonometry).
4. **Cover probability properly.** A short, focused block should lift it quickly, as it has had little attention so far.
5. **Past-paper practice.** Start mixed topic questions and timed sections so that [S1] gets used to exam conditions.
6. **Re-test at week 12** using the same style of test, now including statistics, to measure progress again.

## How parents can help

- **Aim for 6 out of 6 homework.** Setting a regular time for it each week makes a real difference. Ratio homework in particular should be short and frequent (10 to 15 minutes), not left to the night before.
- **Encourage [S1] to bring ratio questions to the session** rather than skipping them. Saying "I don't get this one" is a good sign, not a bad one.
- **Keep attendance as high as it has been.**
