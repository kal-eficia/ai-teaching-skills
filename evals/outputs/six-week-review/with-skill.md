**Assumptions:** the week 1 and week 6 tests are the same style and difficulty, each topic is scored as a percentage of that topic's marks, and "week 6" means the end of this review period. I haven't assumed anything about school lessons or other support, so the table shows what changed, not what caused it.

## 2. Analysis for the teacher

**What moved (keep doing what worked).**
- Algebra (+35) and geometry (+25) account for most of the gain. Whatever the sessions have been doing on these, keep it going, and keep retrieving it so it doesn't fade.
- Number (+15) went up from an already decent start.

**What didn't move, and why.**
- **Ratio and proportion (+5).** [S1] calls it "confusing" and avoids it. This is probably a confidence and avoidance problem on top of a content gap, not just "not taught yet". Avoidance means less practice, which means it stays confusing. A topic that gets skipped won't improve on its own. It is also worth watching because ratio and proportion underpins a lot of GCSE questions, including in geometry, probability and statistics, so a weak spot here can quietly cap marks elsewhere. I'd treat it as a diagnosis job first: find out whether the problem is the idea of ratio, sharing in a ratio, direct and inverse proportion, or reading wordy questions.
- **Probability (0).** Flat, not worse. Without more information I can't say whether it has been taught. If it hasn't been covered yet, flat is exactly what you'd expect. If it has, ask whether it was taught but not retrieved.

**New gap.** None identified. Statistics at 65% is encouraging. Because it has no baseline, treat it as a starting point and re-test it next time.

**Habits.**
- Attendance: 11 of 12 sessions (92%). Very good, and a reason to trust the results.
- Homework: 4 of 6 (67%). Decent but not secure. Given that ratio is being avoided, it is worth checking whether the two missed homeworks were ratio-heavy.
- The "avoids ratio" comment is the most important piece of habit data here. If you can, add a simple confidence rating (1 to 5) per topic next time, so you can see confidence moving alongside scores.

**Honest caveats.**
- This is two data points on a test each time, and a single test can swing by several marks on a bad day. Gains of 25 and 35 points are well beyond that kind of noise. A gain of 5, or a flat 0, is not distinguishable from noise either way.
- If the tests weren't the same questions or the topics had different numbers of marks, a few points of the movement could be down to the paper. Check this if you can.
- A Year 11 student is also learning in school. The numbers show real improvement, and the attendance and the topic-by-topic pattern are consistent with the tutoring helping, but they can't prove the tutoring alone caused it.

## 3. Three priorities for the next six weeks

1. **Ratio and proportion: break the avoidance.**
   *Action:* start every session with two or three low-stakes, short ratio questions (a quick win first, then one step harder), so it becomes routine and not an event. Diagnose the exact sticking point in the first two weeks (for example, a short quiz of one question per subskill), then teach that subskill directly. Target: from 45% to at least 60%.
2. **Keep the gains in algebra and geometry, and lift geometry further.**
   *Action:* put algebra and geometry on a spaced retrieval schedule (a few questions each, a week or two apart), so the gain sticks. Add exam-style geometry questions, as that is the lowest of the topics that moved. Target: algebra stays at 70% or above; geometry reaches 65%.
3. **Make homework reliable, and re-test probability.**
   *Action:* agree a small, fixed homework (about 15 to 20 minutes, with ratio built in), with the aim of 6 out of 6. Teach or revisit probability briefly, then re-test it alongside a repeat of statistics. Target: homework 6 of 6; probability above 50%.

**Next review:** repeat all six topics in the same style at week 12, and add a confidence rating per topic.
