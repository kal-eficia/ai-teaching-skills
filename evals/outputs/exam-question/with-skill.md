# Enzymes: exam-style question (AQA GCSE Combined Science: Biology, Foundation Tier)

**Total: 8 marks | Suggested time: about 8 minutes**

## Assumptions
- Spec area: AQA Trilogy Biology, Topic 2 Organisation (4.2.2 digestion and enzymes: lock and key model, amylase, effect of pH; links to the required practical on pH and amylase). Foundation tier, so the "explain" parts are 3 marks each, with clear linked points available.
- The question is original, not copied from a past paper. Data are invented for practice.
- No teaching-context file was used, and no multiple-choice items are included, so no answer-pattern check was needed.

---

## Question

A student investigated how pH affects the enzyme amylase. Amylase breaks down starch.

**Method**
1. Put 5 cm³ of starch solution and 2 cm³ of buffer solution into a test tube.
2. Add 2 cm³ of amylase solution and start a stopwatch.
3. Every 30 seconds, take a drop of the mixture and add it to iodine solution on a spotting tile.
4. Stop the stopwatch when the iodine solution stays orange-brown (no starch left).
5. Repeat at different pH values. Keep the temperature at 37 °C.

**Results**

| pH of buffer | Time for all the starch to be broken down (seconds) |
|---|---|
| 2 | No change after 600 s |
| 5 | 240 |
| 7 | 60 |
| 9 | 330 |

**(a)** Amylase breaks down starch into a sugar. Name this sugar. **[1]**

**(b)** Which pH gave the fastest breakdown of starch? **[1]**

**(c)** At pH 2 the starch was not broken down at all.
Explain why the amylase did not work at pH 2. **[3]**

**(d)** Amylase breaks down starch but it cannot break down protein.
Explain why. **[3]**

*(Students' hint, optional to print: for "explain" questions, say what happens, then use the word "because" or "so" to say why.)*

---

## Mark scheme

### (a) [1 mark]
- Maltose (1)

**Accept:** glucose (the spec says amylase produces sugars; maltose is the usual answer in school practicals, glucose is the final product after maltase).
**Do not accept:** "sugar" on its own (the question asks you to name it); starch; sucrose; "glycogen".

### (b) [1 mark]
- pH 7 (1)

**Accept:** neutral.
**Do not accept:** 60 (that is the time, not the pH).

### (c) [3 marks]
Marking points (any 3, but the answer must be linked):
1. pH 2 is too acidic / the pH is not the optimum for the enzyme (1)
2. so the shape of the active site changes / the enzyme is denatured (1)
3. so the starch (substrate) no longer fits into the active site (1)
4. so no (or very little) starch is broken down / no reaction takes place (1)

**Accept:** "enzyme is denatured" for point 2; "lock and key no longer works" for point 3; "substrate cannot bind" for point 3.
**Do not accept:**
- "The enzyme is killed" (enzymes are not alive).
- "The enzyme is used up" (enzymes are not used up).
- "The enzyme melts" (this is about heat, not pH).
- Marks for point 2 or 3 if the answer says only "it doesn't work" with no reason.

**Model answer (3 marks):** The acid at pH 2 changes the shape of the active site, so the enzyme is denatured. This means the starch no longer fits into the active site, so the starch is not broken down.

### (d) [3 marks]
Marking points (any 3):
1. Enzymes have a specific shape / the active site has a specific shape (1)
2. Protein (substrate) is a different shape from starch / does not fit the active site of amylase (1)
3. so amylase cannot bind to protein / no enzyme-substrate complex forms (1)
4. so no reaction happens / protein is not broken down (1)

**Accept:** "lock and key" described correctly (substrate fits the enzyme like a key fits a lock); "only complementary substrate fits".
**Do not accept:** "amylase only works on starch" with no reason (this repeats the question); "protein is too big" (not the reason given in the spec); "protease is needed" without any link to shape (extra detail but no mark by itself).

**Model answer (3 marks):** Each enzyme has an active site with a specific shape. The shape of protein does not match the active site of amylase, so protein cannot fit into it. This means amylase cannot break protein down.

## Quick check against the skill checklist
- Command words match marks: "Name" [1], "Which" [1], "Explain" [3] x2. Each explain part has four creditable linked points, three needed.
- Each mark is independently creditable: yes, one point per mark.
- Nothing outside the spec: only enzymes, active site, denaturing, specificity and the pH/amylase practical.
- Difficulty rises: recall, data reading, then two explains.

## Teaching notes for "explain" questions (optional)
Students often lose marks by stating a fact and stopping. A simple scaffold that matches this mark scheme:
- **Point** (what happens): "The enzyme is denatured."
- **Link word**: "because" / "so".
- **Reason** (why it matters): "the substrate no longer fits the active site."

Ask students to underline the linking word in their own answers before handing in. Marking (c) with the chain pH changes, then shape of active site changes, then substrate does not fit, then no reaction helps them see that each step is a mark.
