# AQA GCSE Biology (Combined Science, Foundation Tier): Enzymes

**Total: 8 marks | Suggested time: about 8 to 10 minutes**

*Assumptions: this is set at Foundation level on the AQA topic "Enzymes" (Biology, Topic 4.2 Organisation: digestion and the lock and key model, effect of temperature). The question is built to practise "explain" by moving from recall, to describe, to explain in steps.*

---

## Question

Amylase is an enzyme found in saliva. It breaks down starch.

A student investigated how temperature affects the rate at which amylase breaks down starch.

**Method**
1. The student mixed starch solution and amylase solution in a test tube.
2. The test tube was placed in a water bath at a set temperature.
3. Every 10 seconds, the student took a drop of the mixture and added it to iodine solution.
4. Iodine solution turns blue-black when starch is present.
5. The student recorded the time taken for the iodine to stop turning blue-black.
6. The student repeated this at different temperatures.

**Results**

| Temperature in °C | Time taken for starch to be completely broken down in seconds |
|:---:|:---:|
| 10 | 240 |
| 20 | 120 |
| 30 | 60 |
| 40 | 30 |
| 50 | 90 |
| 60 | Starch was not broken down |

**(a)** Amylase breaks down starch into smaller molecules.
Name **one** of the smaller molecules produced. **[1 mark]**

**(b)** Describe what the results show about the time taken to break down starch between 10 °C and 40 °C. **[2 marks]**

**(c)** Explain why the time taken to break down starch changes between 10 °C and 40 °C. **[2 marks]**

**(d)** Starch was **not** broken down at 60 °C.
Explain why. **[3 marks]**

---

## Mark scheme

**(a) [1 mark]**

| Answer | Marks |
|---|---|
| (a) sugar(s) **or** glucose **or** maltose | 1 |

*Do not accept "amino acids" or "fatty acids". "Sugar" alone is enough.*

**(b) [2 marks]**

| Answer | Marks |
|---|---|
| time (taken) decreases / gets shorter / goes down as temperature increases | 1 |
| uses figures from the table, e.g. from 240 s (at 10 °C) to 30 s (at 40 °C) **or** the time halves with each 10 °C rise | 1 |

*Allow "starch is broken down faster / the rate increases" for the first mark.*
*Do not accept "it gets quicker" with no reference to time or rate for the second mark unless data are used.*

**(c) [2 marks]**

| Answer | Marks |
|---|---|
| as temperature increases, enzyme and substrate (starch) particles move faster / have more (kinetic) energy | 1 |
| so more frequent collisions (between enzyme and substrate) / more enzyme-substrate complexes form in a given time | 1 |

*Allow "more successful collisions".*
*Do not accept "enzymes get more energy" on its own for the first mark if the idea of movement or collisions is missing from the answer as a whole.*
*Do not accept "enzymes are alive" or "enzymes get used up".*

**(d) [3 marks]**

| Answer | Marks |
|---|---|
| the temperature is too high / above the optimum | 1 |
| the shape of the active site changes (the enzyme is denatured) | 1 |
| so the substrate (starch) no longer fits / cannot bind to the active site, so no enzyme-substrate complex forms | 1 |

*Allow "bonds holding the enzyme together break" for the shape change mark.*
*Do not accept "the enzyme is killed" or "the enzyme dies". Enzymes are not alive.*
*Do not accept "the enzyme melts".*
*Do not accept "the substrate changes shape".*
*Allow "the enzyme is denatured" for 1 mark only if the idea of shape or fit is not given elsewhere; the shape and fit points must both be made for full marks.*

---

## Model answers (for students)

**(c)** As the temperature goes up, the starch and amylase particles move faster, **so** they collide more often, **so** more enzyme-substrate complexes form each second and the starch is broken down quicker.

**(d)** 60 °C is too hot. **This** changes the shape of the active site (the amylase is denatured), **so** the starch no longer fits into it and no enzyme-substrate complex can form.

## Teacher notes: helping students with "explain" questions

**Why students lose marks.** "Describe" asks what happens. "Explain" asks **why** it happens, so every mark needs a *because / so* link. Students often restate the trend (describe) and never give the reason. In this question, (b) is describe and (c) and (d) are explain, which lets students see the difference on the same data.

**A simple chain to teach: Point, Because, So.**
1. **Point:** what is happening (e.g. "the rate increases")
2. **Because:** the science reason (e.g. "the particles have more kinetic energy and move faster")
3. **So:** the result that links back to the question (e.g. "there are more collisions, so more products are made per second")

**Sentence starters to put on the board**
- "This happens because..."
- "...which means that..."
- "...so the substrate can no longer..."

**Common misconceptions to listen for**
- "The enzyme is killed / dies." Enzymes are proteins, not living things. Use "denatured".
- "The enzyme melts." No. The shape of the active site changes.
- "The enzyme changes shape so it works slower." At 60 °C it does not work at all, because the substrate no longer fits.
- "Enzymes use up energy / the enzyme is used up." Enzymes are not used up in the reaction.
- Using "time" and "rate" as the same thing. A shorter time means a **faster** rate.

**Use as a retrieval or peer-marking task.** Give students the mark scheme for (c) and (d) and ask them to underline the *because* and the *so* in their own answer. If they cannot find both, they have only described.

**Extension (not marked).** Ask: "Predict the time at 45 °C. Explain why it is hard to predict from this table." (Answer: the optimum is somewhere between 40 °C and 50 °C and the enzyme starts to denature, so you cannot tell from these data alone.)

**Practical link (AQA required practical context).** This links to the investigation into the effect of pH on the rate of amylase activity. The same "explain" structure works for pH: change in pH, shape of the active site changes, substrate no longer fits.
