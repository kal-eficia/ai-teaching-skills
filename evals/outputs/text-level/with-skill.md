## Rewritten text

===
**Tropical rainforests**

Tropical rainforests have a huge variety of living things. This variety of life is called **biodiversity**. Rainforests have so much of it because they are always hot. They also get a lot of rain. Over 2,000 millimetres of rain falls every year. That is more than two metres.

**The layers of a rainforest**

A rainforest is split into four layers. Each layer is called a different name:

- **Emergent layer:** the very tallest trees poke out above everything else.
- **Canopy:** the thick roof of leaves and branches below them.
- **Understorey:** the shady middle zone under the canopy.
- **Forest floor:** the ground at the bottom.

Each layer has its own special plants and animals. They have adapted to live there. This means their bodies and ways of life are suited to the place. The layers are different in how much light they get. They are also different in how damp the air is. Plants and animals live in the layer that suits them best.

**Deforestation**

Rainforests are in danger from **deforestation**. This means cutting down large areas of forest. People do this for three main reasons:

- to make space for big farms that grow crops and raise animals to sell (commercial agriculture)
- to cut down trees for wood (logging)
- to build roads, towns and other buildings (infrastructure)

Deforestation causes big problems:

- It breaks the forest into small pieces. This is called **habitat fragmentation**. Animals and plants lose their homes and find it hard to move around.
- Some species are **endemic**. This means they live in one place only and nowhere else in the world. If their forest goes, they can be lost for ever.
- Trees store **carbon** in their wood and leaves. When trees are cut down, this carbon goes back into the air.

This extra carbon in the air makes **climate change** worse. This kind of climate change is caused by people.
===

## Glossary

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Tropical rainforest | A thick forest in a hot, wet part of the world |
| Biodiversity | The variety of different living things in a place |
| Emergent layer | The top layer, where the tallest trees stick out above the rest |
| Canopy | The thick roof of leaves and branches |
| Understorey | The shady layer under the canopy |
| Forest floor | The ground layer of the rainforest |
| Adapted | Suited to a place, so a plant or animal can live well there |
| Deforestation | Cutting down large areas of forest |
| Commercial agriculture | Farming to sell crops or animals for money |
| Logging | Cutting down trees for wood |
| Infrastructure | Things people build, such as roads and buildings |
| Habitat fragmentation | A habitat broken up into small, separate pieces |
| Endemic | Found in one place only and nowhere else |
| Carbon | A substance stored in trees and released into the air when they are cut down |
| Climate change | A long-term change in the world's weather patterns |
