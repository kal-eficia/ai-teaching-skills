Tropical rainforests are home to a huge number of different plants and animals. This is called biodiversity. There are so many living things here because rainforests are hot all year round. They also get a lot of rain. More than 2,000 millimetres of rain falls every year.

A rainforest grows in four layers. Each layer is like a different floor in a tall building:

- The emergent layer is at the very top. The tallest trees poke out above everything else.
- The canopy is the thick roof of leaves and branches below them.
- The understorey is the darker, shadier layer under the canopy.
- The forest floor is the ground at the bottom.

Every layer has its own special plants and animals. They have changed over time to suit where they live. Some layers get lots of light and some get very little. Some are damper than others.

People are cutting down rainforests. This is called deforestation. There are three main reasons for it:

- Big farms clear land to grow crops and raise animals to sell.
- Logging companies cut down trees for wood.
- People clear land to build roads and other things, like towns.

Deforestation is a big danger for rainforests. It breaks the forest into small patches. This means animals lose their homes, which are called habitats. Some animals and plants only live in one place in the world. These are called endemic species. If their forest is cut down, they could disappear for ever.

Trees also store carbon. When the trees are cut down, this carbon goes back into the air. This makes climate change worse. It is called human-caused climate change, because people are making it happen.
