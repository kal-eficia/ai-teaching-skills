# Year 8 Science: Starter for tomorrow (Recall Grid)

**Time:** 6 to 8 minutes. Silent, written, closed book, no talking.
**Purpose:** Retrieval practice before the acids and alkalis lesson. Two questions target last week's quiz gaps (compounds vs mixtures; what happens to particles when a solid melts). The rest is spaced recall from the last four lessons, the particle model and cells.

**Assumptions:** 50 to 60 minute lesson, mini whiteboards or the back of exercise books, you read each question aloud or show it on the board. Students attempt every question before any answers are given.

---

## On the board

**Title:** *Think hard, no notes. Wrong answers now help your brain more than not trying.*

Students write numbers 1 to 6 and answer in a few words each.

### 1. Compound or mixture? (quiz gap)
Sodium chloride is made of sodium and chlorine atoms joined together by chemical bonds. Seawater is salt and water that are simply mixed.
Which one is a **compound** and which is a **mixture**? Give **one** reason for each.

### 2. Melting (quiz gap)
A solid is heated until it melts. Describe what happens to the **particles**. Include **two** things: what happens to their **movement** and what happens to their **arrangement**.

### 3. Particle diagram (particle model, 3 weeks ago)
Draw a quick box showing the particles in a **liquid**. Add one label that tells me how they are arranged.

### 4. Sorting (lessons 1 to 3)
Write **C** (chemical change) or **P** (physical change) for each:

- (a) Wax melting
- (b) Iron rusting
- (c) Dissolving sugar in tea
- (d) Burning magnesium ribbon

### 5. Word equation (yesterday)
Magnesium reacts with oxygen to make magnesium oxide.
Write the word equation. Then circle the **reactants**.

### 6. Cells (a month ago)
Name **one** structure found in a plant cell but **not** in an animal cell, and say what it does.

---

## Answers (show after students have committed to every answer)

1. **Sodium chloride is the compound.** Its atoms are chemically bonded, it has fixed properties and it cannot be separated by simple methods like filtering or evaporating. **Seawater is the mixture.** It is not chemically joined, so it can be separated physically (for example by evaporation) and the amounts can vary.
2. Particles gain energy, so they **vibrate more and move around faster**. They move from a **fixed, regular arrangement to being able to slide past each other**, still close together. (Also accept: the forces holding them in place are overcome. Do **not** accept "the particles melt" or "the particles get bigger".)
3. Particles drawn **touching or very close, in a random/irregular arrangement, not in a fixed pattern**. Label such as "close together but can move past each other".
4. (a) **P** (b) **C** (c) **P** (d) **C**
5. **magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide.** Reactants to circle: **magnesium** and **oxygen**.
6. Any one of: **cell wall** (supports and strengthens the cell); **chloroplasts** (contain chlorophyll, where photosynthesis happens); **permanent vacuole** (contains cell sap, keeps the cell firm).

---

## Quick teacher notes

- **How to run it:** Give about 90 seconds per question for Q1 and Q2, less for the others. Then go through the answers. Students tick or correct in a different colour pen, because the correction is where the learning happens.
- **Q1 and Q2 are the priority.** Spend the most time on these two. For Q1, hold up two labelled things if you have them (iron filings and sulfur mixed, versus iron sulfide) and ask what you could do to separate each. If many miss Q2, draw the solid, then liquid particle boxes side by side on the board and ask students to describe the change in their own words.
- **Common mistakes to listen for:**
  - "A compound is when you mix two things." Redirect: mixing does not make a new substance; bonding does.
  - "The particles melt" or "the particles get bigger" when a solid melts.
  - Listing the **product** as a reactant in Q5.
  - Confusing dissolving (physical, the sugar can be recovered) with a chemical change.
- **Bridge into the new topic (30 seconds):** Say: "Burning magnesium and rusting are chemical changes that make new substances. Today we meet a group of substances that can cause chemical changes just by touching things: acids and alkalis." Then move straight into the lesson.
- **If time is short:** Drop Q3 and Q6, keep Q1, Q2, Q4 and Q5.
- **Follow-up:** Note which students missed Q1 or Q2 and re-ask a variant of each at the start of a lesson next week (spacing the retrieval will help it stick).
