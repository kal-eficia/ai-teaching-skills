# Year 8 Science: Starter for tomorrow (acids and alkalis)

**Assumptions:** 8 questions, about 6 to 7 minutes, done on paper or mini-whiteboards. No new content (acids and alkalis is not touched). The class's two weak spots from last week's quiz (compounds vs mixtures, and particles during melting) each get a second, differently worded question, because one retry is rarely enough. Cells is included as a "never retrieved since" question.

---

## Student starter

**No notes. No searching. No AI. Answer from memory. A wrong answer now helps you more than a copied one.**

**1.** Which of these is a **mixture**, not a compound?
- A. Carbon dioxide
- B. Sodium chloride
- C. Salty water
- D. Water

**2.** A solid melts into a liquid. What happens to the **particles**? (Write one or two sentences.)

**3.** Look at this word equation: magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide. Which substances are the **reactants**?
- A. Magnesium and oxygen
- B. Magnesium oxide only
- C. Oxygen and magnesium oxide
- D. Magnesium and magnesium oxide

**4.** Which part of a cell controls what goes in and out of it?
- A. Nucleus
- B. Cytoplasm
- C. Mitochondria
- D. Cell membrane

**5.** Give **one** difference between a compound and a mixture. (Think: how are the substances joined, and how easy is it to separate them?)

**6.** Which of these is a **chemical** change?
- A. Ice melting
- B. Wood burning
- C. Sugar dissolving in tea
- D. Water boiling

**7.** Which sentence describes the particles in a **gas**?
- A. Close together and vibrating on the spot
- B. Close together and sliding past each other
- C. Far apart and moving quickly in all directions
- D. Far apart and not moving

**8.** Name the group of the periodic table that contains the unreactive gases (such as helium and neon).

*For Q1, 3, 4, 6 and 7, also write: Guessing / Think so / Certain.*

---

## Teacher key

MCQ answer key: **C, A, D, B, C** for Q1, 3, 4, 6, 7. Checked with the no-answer-patterns script: clean (no repeats, runs, staircases, alternation, repeating blocks or lopsided spread).

| # | Answer | Topic | Why it is here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **C** Salty water. (Distractors are all compounds.) | Compounds vs mixtures | Priority 1: most of the class confused these in last week's quiz. |
| 2 | Particles gain energy, vibrate more and the forces holding them in fixed positions are overcome, so they can move past each other. They stay close together. Accept "they move more / start to slide past each other". Watch for "the particles melt" or "they get bigger" (misconception). | Particle model (3 weeks ago) | Priority 1 and 2: about half the class could not say this, and it is due a spaced check at about 3 weeks. |
| 3 | **A** Magnesium and oxygen. (Common wrong answer: D or B, from reading the arrow the wrong way.) | Word equations (yesterday) | Priority 3: keeps the thread from last lesson. |
| 4 | **D** Cell membrane. | Cells (a month ago) | Priority 4 and 2: not retrieved since, now at the 30-day mark. |
| 5 | Compound: two or more elements chemically joined, properties differ from the elements, hard to separate (needs a chemical reaction). Mixture: substances just mixed, not chemically joined, keep their own properties, easy to separate by physical methods (filtering, evaporating etc.). Accept any one clear difference. | Compounds vs mixtures (second angle) | Priority 1: asks for the explanation, not just recognition. Compare with Q1: if a student gets Q1 right but Q5 wrong, they are pattern-matching examples without the idea. |
| 6 | **B** Wood burning. (Look for melting/boiling/dissolving picked: that is the physical vs chemical confusion.) | Chemical vs physical changes (lesson 3) | Priority 3: the lesson between the two you most want to secure. Also lets you see whether "new substance made" is secure. |
| 7 | **C** Far apart and moving quickly in all directions. | Particle model (3 weeks ago) | Priority 2: a second, easier particle question so students who failed Q2 can still succeed on part of the topic. |
| 8 | Group 0 (noble gases). Accept "Group 8" if your school uses that numbering. | Periodic table groups (lesson 2) | Priority 3 and 2: short recall, ends the starter on a quick win before the new topic. |

**Interleaving:** no two neighbouring questions share a topic (mixtures, particles, equations, cells, mixtures, chemical change, particles, periodic table).

**Using the confidence ratings:** look first at "Certain" with a wrong answer on Q1, 3, 4, 6, 7. Confident and wrong on Q1 means the compound/mixture misconception is firmly held and needs a short re-teach, not just a recap.

**Suggested follow-up (2 minutes):** go through Q2 and Q5 aloud with the class, since they were the two known weak points. Keep the other answers quick. Then link to the new topic: tomorrow's acids and alkalis uses "chemical change" and "word equations" (acid + alkali reactions), so Q3 and Q6 are the warm-up.
