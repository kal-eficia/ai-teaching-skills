# Why we have seasons: Year 7 explanation

**Assumptions:** UK class, about 15 to 20 minutes, a globe or ball and a torch available (a lamp or phone torch works too). Written so you can read it out or adapt it. British spelling and metric units used.

---

## 1. Start with the idea they already have

Ask: **"Hands up if you think it's hotter in summer because the Earth is closer to the Sun?"**

Tell them it's a really sensible guess. Things *are* hotter when you stand closer to them (a campfire, a radiator). So the idea makes sense. It just turns out to be wrong, and we can prove it with two facts.

### Proof 1: Australia
When it's summer in the UK, it's **winter** in Australia. Both countries are on the same planet at the same moment, so they are the same distance from the Sun. If distance caused the seasons, everyone on Earth would have summer at the same time. They don't.

### Proof 2: The real distances
Earth's orbit is only very slightly squashed from a perfect circle.

| When | Distance from the Sun |
|---|---|
| Early January (UK winter) | about 147 million km (closest) |
| Early July (UK summer) | about 152 million km (furthest) |

So the Earth is actually **closest to the Sun in the middle of the UK winter**. The difference is about 3%, which is far too small to change the weather much.

---

## 2. The real reason: the Earth is tilted

The Earth spins on an imaginary line through the North and South Poles, called its **axis**. That axis is **not upright**. It leans over by about **23.5 degrees**.

The key facts:

- The tilt always points the **same way in space** (towards the same star, called Polaris), all year round.
- The Earth takes **one year** to travel around the Sun.
- Because the tilt stays pointing the same way while we move round, sometimes the **north** half leans **towards** the Sun and sometimes it leans **away**.

**Summer in the UK:** the northern half of the Earth is tipped towards the Sun.
**Winter in the UK:** the northern half is tipped away from the Sun.

The southern half does the opposite, which is why Australia's seasons are the other way round to ours.

---

## 3. Why does tipping towards the Sun make it hotter?

There are two effects working together.

### Effect 1: Stronger sunlight
When your half of the Earth leans towards the Sun, the Sun's rays hit the ground more **directly** (almost from straight above). The energy is packed into a small patch of ground, so it heats it up strongly.

When your half leans away, the rays hit at a low, slanted angle. The same amount of light is **spread over a much bigger area**, so each bit of ground gets less heat.

*Picture it:* Shine a torch straight down at a table and you get a small, bright circle. Tilt the torch and the light stretches into a big, dim oval. Same torch, same energy, but weaker light in each spot.

### Effect 2: Longer days
When your half is tipped towards the Sun, you spend more of each 24-hour spin in sunlight. In the UK in June, the Sun is up for about **16 to 17 hours**. In December it's only about **8 hours**. More hours of sunshine means more time to warm up, and less time to cool down overnight.

**Strong sunlight plus long days = summer. Weak sunlight plus short days = winter.**

---

## 4. Quick demo (3 minutes)

You need: a globe or ball with a mark for the UK, and a torch.

1. Put the torch on a desk to be the Sun. Hold the globe tilted, with the tilt pointing at the same wall corner all the time.
2. Walk the globe round the torch in a slow circle, keeping the tilt pointing the same way.
3. Stop at four points. At each one, ask: **"Is the UK leaning towards the torch or away from it?"**
4. At the "towards" point, look at how bright and small the lit patch on the UK is. At the "away" point, look at how it spreads out and fades.

Common slip to watch for: pupils (and teachers!) letting the tilt swing round as they walk. The axis must keep pointing the **same direction**.

---

## 5. The four seasons in one table

| Season (UK) | Which way is the north tipped? | Sunlight | Day length |
|---|---|---|---|
| Summer (June) | Towards the Sun | Strong, direct | Long |
| Autumn (September) | Sideways, in between | Getting weaker | Getting shorter |
| Winter (December) | Away from the Sun | Weak, slanted | Short |
| Spring (March) | Sideways, in between | Getting stronger | Getting longer |

**Bonus fact:** At the **equator** the Sun is always high in the sky, so the weather stays warm all year and there are no proper four seasons. The tilt matters most the further you are from the equator.

---

## 6. Check understanding (mini quiz)

Ask pupils to answer on mini whiteboards, then reveal.

1. **True or false:** The Earth is closest to the Sun in the UK summer.
   *False. It's closest in early January.*
2. **What is it called when we say the Earth is leaning over by 23.5 degrees?**
   *The tilt of its axis.*
3. **It is summer in Brazil's neighbour, Argentina, in December. What season is it in the UK, and why?**
   *Winter. The UK's half of the Earth is tipped away from the Sun.*
4. **Give two reasons summer is warmer than winter in the UK.**
   *The Sun's rays are more direct (stronger), and the days are longer.*
5. **If the Earth had no tilt at all, what would happen to the seasons?**
   *There would be almost no seasons. Each place would get about the same sunlight all year.*

---

## 7. Misconceptions to listen out for

- **"The tilt changes during the year."** No. The tilt stays the same. What changes is *where in the orbit* the Earth is, so the tilt points towards or away from the Sun at different times.
- **"Summer is when the Sun is closer."** Covered above. Use the Australia proof.
- **"The Earth's tilt is what makes day and night."** No. Day and night come from the Earth *spinning* once every 24 hours.

---

## 8. One-sentence summary for their books

> We have seasons because the Earth's axis is tilted by about 23.5 degrees, so over the year each half of the planet leans towards the Sun (stronger sunlight and longer days, so summer) and then away from it (weaker sunlight and shorter days, so winter).
