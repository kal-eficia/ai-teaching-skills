# Cover work: Year 8 Science, Period 3 (50 minutes, 30 students)

**Topic:** The digestive system, organs and their jobs (revision and practice)
**Why this and not enzymes:** You want to teach enzymes yourself, so this lesson only revisits what they have already learned. Nothing new needs explaining, and nobody is left behind when you return to start enzymes.

**Assumptions (change anything that's wrong):**
- Students have already covered the organs and their jobs, so I've assumed these: mouth, salivary glands, oesophagus, stomach, liver, gall bladder, pancreas, small intestine, large intestine, rectum and anus.
- You can photocopy or print. I've written the student sheet to be copied as it is. If you can't print, the cover teacher can write the questions on the board, and I've added a note on that below.
- Students have exercise books and pens, and there is a class set of textbooks. I don't know which textbook, so I haven't given page numbers. Please add the digestion chapter page in the space on the cover sheet.
- No peer marking of the exit question. The cover teacher collects everything.

Please check the answers against how you taught each organ. If you used different wording, edit the answer sheet before printing so the cover teacher isn't confused.

(If starter checking runs over, shorten the main task. Don't skip the exit question.)

**Collect:** All 30 student sheets, with names on. Put them in a pile for the teacher, with a note of anyone absent or anyone who misbehaved.

**Behaviour:** Use the school's normal system. Please leave a list of anyone who didn't work for the teacher.

**If something goes wrong**
- **No photocopies or the printer fails:** Write Sections A and D on the board and ask students to copy the question and answer in their books. For Section B, students draw the table in their books from the board. Section C can be done straight from the textbook.
- **Textbooks missing:** Students use their exercise book notes. The main task can still be done from their notes. If they have no notes, they should write what they remember and leave gaps.
- **Finished very early:** Challenge. If done, they read their textbook digestion pages quietly and write down three facts they didn't know.
- **A student says "we haven't done this":** Tell them to try anyway, and to write "not sure" instead of leaving blanks. The teacher will check.
- **A student says they have already done it:** Give them the Challenge.

(Answers are the organ names. Accept sensible spelling attempts.)

**Section B: Organ table (11 organs, 1 mark each for the job)**

| Organ | Job (accept answers in similar words) |
|---|---|
| Mouth | Teeth chew and break food into smaller pieces. Tongue mixes food and forms it into a ball (bolus). Saliva added. |
| Salivary glands | Make saliva, which moistens food and helps swallowing. |
| Oesophagus | Muscles squeeze food down to the stomach (peristalsis). |
| Stomach | Churns food, mixes it with acid and digestive juices. Acid kills bacteria. |
| Liver | Makes bile. |
| Gall bladder | Stores bile and releases it into the small intestine. |
| Pancreas | Makes digestive juices (which contain enzymes) and releases them into the small intestine. |
| Small intestine | Digestion is finished here. Digested food is absorbed into the blood. |
| Large intestine | Absorbs water from the leftover food. |
| Rectum | Stores faeces. |
| Anus | Faeces leave the body. |

**Section C: Journey of a sandwich (answers)**
Correct order: mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine, large intestine, rectum, anus.
Matching question: accept any answer that follows the table above.
Marking note: it is fine if wording differs, as long as the idea is right.

**Section D: Exit question (3 marks)**
"Explain why the small intestine is the most important organ for getting nutrients into the body." A good answer includes: digestion finishes here, the digested food is absorbed into the blood, and the blood carries it round the body. A good extra point is any mention of the small intestine being long or having a large surface area (don't worry if they don't mention it, because that may not have been taught).

**Challenge (answers)**
Ask students to read their answer to each other if you have time. The teacher will mark it. There is no single correct answer, as long as the explanation is sensible and uses organ names correctly.

# NOTES FOR YOU (the teacher)

- **Section C question 3** has the answer: food never enters the liver. Food passes through the tube (the gut). The liver makes bile, which is released into the small intestine.
- The exit question gives you a quick read on who has the "digestion finishes and absorption happens in the small intestine" idea. That is a handy starting point for your enzymes lesson.
- Look at Section A scores and the exit question first. Anyone scoring under 5 may need a recap before enzymes.
- Please check the textbook page number is filled in before you print.
