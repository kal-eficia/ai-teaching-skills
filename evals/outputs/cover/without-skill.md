# Cover Work: Year 8 Science, Period 3 (50 minutes, 30 students)

**Topic:** The digestive system, consolidating the organs and their jobs
**Cover teacher needs:** No science knowledge. Everything is scripted, and a full answer key is in Part 4.
**Equipment:** Student exercise books, pens, the class textbook, the whiteboard. No computers. A photocopier is helpful but not essential (see "If you can't photocopy").

---

## Notes for the class teacher (before you leave)

Assumptions I've made, so you can adjust quickly:

- **I haven't pre-taught enzymes.** You want to teach those yourself, so this lesson is revision and consolidation of organs and their jobs. Nothing in it needs enzymes. If a student asks "what are enzymes?", the cover has a line to say (below).
- **Textbook pages:** I don't know your book, so fill in the blank on the cover sheet (Part 1) with the page numbers for the digestive system chapter before you go. Tasks work without the book if it isn't suitable, because the fact file can be done from memory and notes.
- **Seating:** Normal seating plan. If you have one, leave it on the desk.
- **Work is collected** in a pile at the end so you can see what was done. Nothing needs marking by the cover teacher.
- It's worth leaving a note for the cover teacher with names of any students who need extra support or who might need separating.

Suggested next lesson: use the exit ticket answers (Task 5) to see what they remember before you start enzymes.

### Timings

| Time | What | What you do |

### Handling the class
- **Expectation:** quiet individual work for tasks 2 to 4. For Task 1 and the plenary, calm talking is fine.
- **Early finishers:** go straight to the extension (Part 3).
- **Students who say they "don't get it":** point them to the textbook diagram and the word bank on the board. They should write something for every row, even if it's short.
- **If a student asks about enzymes:** say, *"That's what your teacher is going to teach you next lesson. For today, we're just doing the organs and their jobs."* They can write the question down at the back of their book for next time.
- **If a student needs the toilet or is unwell:** follow normal school procedure, and one at a time.
- **If anything goes wrong:** report it to the department head or the duty office as per school policy, and leave a note for me.

## Part 2: Student tasks

### Task 1: Starter quiz (8 minutes, from memory, books closed)

Write the answers in your book, numbered 1 to 8. (Cover teacher reads each question aloud twice.)

1. What is the name of the tube that carries food from your mouth to your stomach?
2. Which organ squeezes and churns food and mixes it with acid?
3. Which organ makes bile?
4. In which organ are most of the digested nutrients absorbed into the blood?
5. What happens to the leftover water in the large intestine?
6. Give one job of the teeth.
7. Which organ stores bile until it is needed?
8. True or false: food goes into the liver. Explain your answer in one sentence.

*(Answers are in Part 4. Students mark in a different colour pen.)*

---

### Task 3: Food's journey (12 minutes)

Imagine you have just bitten into a cheese sandwich.

**Draw a flow chart** (boxes and arrows) in your book showing the journey of the food from your mouth to leaving your body. Each box needs:

- the name of the organ
- one thing that happens to the food there

Then, **underneath**, write 3 to 4 sentences that describe the journey in your own words, using at least **five** words from the word bank.

> Careful: draw the organs in the right order. Check against your fact file.

---

### Task 4: Exam-style questions (10 minutes)

Answer in full sentences. Marks are in brackets.

1. Name the **two** parts of the digestive system where food is physically broken into smaller pieces. [2]
2. Explain why the small intestine has a very large surface area (it is long and folded). Use the word **absorb** in your answer. *Hint: think about what the small intestine needs to do with digested food.* [2]
3. The liver and the pancreas are not part of the tube that food travels through. Explain why they are still important for digestion. [2]
4. Put these in the correct order, writing the letters: **A** stomach **B** mouth **C** large intestine **D** oesophagus **E** small intestine [2]
5. A student says: "The large intestine digests most of our food." Is the student correct? Explain your answer. [2]

**Total: 10 marks**

## Part 3: Extension (for early finishers)

1. **Design a poster or a labelled diagram** of the digestive system. Label at least 8 parts and add a one-line job beside each.
2. **Explain it to a Year 6 student.** Write a short explanation (5 sentences) of what happens to food after you swallow it, in simple words.
3. **Challenge question:** Why is it a good thing that food takes many hours to travel through the whole digestive system? Give two reasons.

## Part 4: Answer key (for the cover teacher or the class teacher)

### Task 1
1. Oesophagus (accept gullet).
2. Stomach.
3. Liver.
4. Small intestine.
5. It is absorbed into the blood (accept: taken back into the body).
6. Chew or cut up or grind food into smaller pieces (any one).
7. Gall bladder.
8. **False.** Food does not go into the liver. The liver makes bile, but food travels through the tube (gullet, stomach, intestines).

### Task 2 (accept any sensible wording)

| Organ | What it does | Fact or key word |
|---|---|---|
| Mouth | Teeth chew and break up food. Saliva makes it easier to swallow. | Chewing, saliva, swallowing |
| Oesophagus | Muscles squeeze food down to the stomach. | Peristalsis (muscle squeezing) |
| Stomach | Churns food and mixes it with acid. Kills many bacteria. | Churning, acid |
| Small intestine | Digestion is finished and digested food is absorbed into the blood. | Long and folded with a large surface area, absorption |
| Large intestine | Absorbs water from the leftover food. | Water absorption |
| Rectum and anus | Rectum stores faeces. Faeces leave the body through the anus. | Egestion (faeces) |

Helper organs:
- **Liver:** makes bile.
- **Gall bladder:** stores bile and releases it into the small intestine.
- **Pancreas:** makes digestive juices that go into the small intestine.
- **Salivary glands:** make saliva in the mouth.

### Task 3
Correct order: mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine, large intestine, rectum, anus. Boxes should name the organ and one thing that happens. The written paragraph should use at least five words from the word bank in the right context.

### Task 4
1. Mouth (chewing) and stomach (churning). [1 mark each]
2. A large surface area means more food can be absorbed into the blood faster. [1 for large surface area, 1 for absorb or faster or more]
3. They make substances (bile and digestive juices) that go into the small intestine and help break down food. [1 mark for each organ's contribution, or one overall linked statement for 2 marks]
4. **B, D, A, E, C.** [2 marks; 1 mark if only one error]
5. **No.** Most digestion and absorption happens in the small intestine. The large intestine mainly absorbs water. [1 for no, 1 for explanation]

### Task 5
Any sensible answer. Class teacher: skim these to see what to revisit before starting enzymes.

### Extension
1. Check the order and the jobs against the Task 2 answers.
2. Any clear 5-sentence explanation in simple language.
3. Possible reasons: more time to break food down, more time to absorb nutrients, more time to absorb water. Accept any two sensible reasons.
