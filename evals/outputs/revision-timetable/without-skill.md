# Year 11 Mock Revision Timetable (3 weeks + mock week)

## Assumptions

- Revision starts today and runs for 3 full weeks (Weeks 1 to 3). Mocks begin the Monday of Week 4: Maths Paper 1 (Mon), English Language (Tue), Biology (Wed), History (Thu).
- Exam boards are not given, so topics are kept general (AQA/Edexcel-style GCSE). Swap in the exact topics your school has set.
- Homework still needs doing. This timetable is for revision only, so treat the slots below as the minimum and fit homework around them.
- Dinner is around 6:45 to 7:15 and prayer times are fitted in around the sessions. Move any block that clashes.
- Every session is **25 minutes of work + 5 minutes break** (no phone in the break except for music, no scrolling). This suits a student who can't concentrate for more than half an hour. If a student is flagging, drop to 20 minutes. Do not push to 40.
- Maths gets the most time, history second, then biology and English.

## How to revise (the student has never done this, so teach it first)

Re-reading notes and highlighting feels like revision but barely works. Use these four methods instead, in this order of importance.

1. **Retrieval practice (most important).** Close the book and write or say everything you can remember. Then check what you missed. Struggling to remember is what builds memory.
2. **Past questions.** Especially for maths. Do a question, mark it, redo the ones you got wrong two days later.
3. **Flashcards with spacing.** Use a box or an app (Anki, Quizlet). Cards you get right move back a day or two. Cards you get wrong come back tomorrow.
4. **Blurting and mind-maps.** Write everything you know on a blank page, then add what you missed in a different colour.

**Start of every session (2 minutes):** write the topic and one goal on a sticky note, e.g. "Factorising quadratics: do 8 questions and get 6 right."
**End of every session (2 minutes):** write one line on what stuck and one thing still shaky. That list feeds Sunday's review.

## Maths (weakest: algebra and ratio)

Maths Paper 1 is the first exam, so it gets the first and most frequent slots. In every maths session, do **questions, not reading**. Watch a short video (Corbettmaths, Maths Genie, Dr Frost, Sparx) for 5 minutes at most, then spend the rest practising.

| Week | Algebra focus | Ratio focus |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Simplifying, expanding brackets, solving linear equations, substitution | Simplifying ratios, sharing in a ratio, ratio to fractions |
| **2** | Factorising (single bracket, then quadratics), rearranging formulae, inequalities | Proportion, best buy, recipe-style problems, ratio and percentages |
| **3** | Simultaneous equations, sequences, mixed algebra exam questions | Mixed ratio and proportion, then full Paper 1 style question sets |

**Rules for maths sessions**
- Do 8 to 10 questions per session. Mark straight away with the answers.
- Anything wrong goes on a **"redo list"**. Redo those questions two days later, with no notes.
- Week 3: do at least one **timed past paper** (or half a paper) on the Saturday or Sunday, under quiet conditions. Calculator and non-calculator practice both matter if Paper 1 is non-calculator.
- Spend any "free" minutes on the quickest wins: topics where the student is nearly right.

## History (weak: remembering dates and events)

The problem is memory, so the fix is **spaced retrieval**, not more reading.

**Method: build one timeline for each topic**
1. For each topic, pick **8 to 10 key dates** to learn per week. Fewer is better. Only the dates that matter.
2. Make each flashcard: front = the date or the event, back = what happened + **why it mattered** (one line). Attaching the "why" makes it stick.
3. Put the dates on a **single-page timeline** on the wall or bedroom door. Seeing it daily helps.
4. Tuesday and Wednesday 20 to 25 minute sessions are **flashcards only**. Say the answer out loud before turning the card over.
5. Link events in chains: "X happened, which caused Y, which led to Z."

| Week | Focus |
|---|---|
| **1** | Build the timeline and flashcards for the first half of the course. Learn the first 8 to 10 dates. Test yourself on Friday. |
| **2** | Learn the second half. Re-test week 1 dates every session (spaced repetition). Start 1 to 2 short answer paragraphs using dates as evidence. |
| **3** | Full timeline recall from a blank page. Practise the exam question types (e.g. "explain why", "how significant"). Past questions with a plan, not full essays. |

**Memory tricks that work:** mnemonics for lists of causes or events, linking a date to a number you already know, and drawing a tiny picture on each card.

## Biology

Four sessions' worth of material each week, but only 3 slots, so keep it tight.

| Week | Focus |
|---|---|
| **1** | Cell biology and organisation. Make a one-page summary of each topic from the textbook or revision guide, then blurt it from memory. |
| **2** | Infection and response, bioenergetics (photosynthesis and respiration). Learn key terms with flashcards. Do exam questions on each. |
| **3** | Cover whatever topics are on the mock. Past-paper questions. Practise 6-mark answers and required practicals. |

Use **key word lists** (e.g. diffusion, osmosis, active transport) and say each definition out loud. Biology marks are won by using exact scientific terms.

---

## English Language

Not a content subject, so the aim is **skill practice**, little and often.

| Week | Focus |
|---|---|
| **1** | Read and annotate one unseen extract. Practise finding quotes, then explaining the effect of language choices (use PEE or PEEL paragraphs). |
| **2** | Short timed answers (10 to 15 min) for each question type. Plan one writing task (description or story): plan in 5 minutes, write for 25. |
| **3** | One full timed paper on the weekend. Reread feedback from earlier assessments. Practise ambitious vocabulary and accurate punctuation (the quick marks). |

Also do **10 minutes of reading daily** (a news article, a novel, anything). It improves both comprehension and writing, with no extra "revision" time needed.

## Weekly review (Sunday, 25 minutes)

Every Sunday, spend one session:
1. Reading the "one thing still shaky" notes from the week.
2. Re-doing the maths redo list.
3. Quizzing yourself on 10 history dates from a blank page.
4. Choosing next week's top three priorities.

## Practical rules for the student and parents

- **One place, one desk, phone in another room** (not just face down). Phones are the biggest cause of lost concentration at this age.
- **Study in the same slot each day** so it becomes routine.
- **Sleep:** at least 8 to 9 hours. Memory is made overnight.
- **Eat and drink water** before sessions. Football days are fine to keep as they are. Exercise helps memory and mood. Keep both clubs.
- **Missed a session?** Don't double up. Move on to the next one and make up the gap on Sunday.
- **Reward finishing:** a tick on a wall chart for each session done. A visible streak helps a first-time reviser stay motivated.

## A note for the teacher

If the student's mock results are well below target after the first week of practice, ask for the specific topics they are getting wrong. The timetable is built to be adjusted: swap in the topics that come back wrong, and cut anything already secure. Small and regular beats long and rare.
