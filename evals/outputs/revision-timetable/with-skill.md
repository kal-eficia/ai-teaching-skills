# Year 11 Mock Revision Timetable: 3 weeks to Mocks

Mocks: **Maths Paper 1 (Mon, week 4)**, **English Language (Tue)**, **Biology (Wed)**, **History (Thu)**.

## Assumptions (change any of these)

- Revision starts **Monday of Week 1**. Weeks 1 to 3 are revision, Week 4 is mock week.
- Short blocks: **30 minutes (25 minutes work, 5 minutes break)**, because the student can't focus for longer. Blocks are never back to back until Week 3, and then only with a 10-minute break.
- Times are suggestions. Slide them around prayers, dinner and family. Fixed commitments come first: school to 3:15, football Tue/Thu 5:00 to 6:30, mosque class Sat 10:00 to 12:00.
- Maths Paper 1 is taken to be the non-calculator paper. Topic names for Biology, History and English are typical ones. **Swap in your exam board's actual mock content.** History topics are shown as H1, H2, H3 (your three units in date order, or the three biggest topics on the mock).
- Weighting: Maths about 36%, History about 27%, Biology about 18%, English about 9%, catch-up about 9% to 10%. Maths and History are weighted up because they're the weakest areas. English Language is mostly skills, so it needs fewer, sharper blocks.
- Friday is the lighter day (one block). Sunday afternoon and evening are fully off after the check-in.
- The student has never revised before, so Week 1 teaches the method while covering content. No block is "read your notes" on its own.

## How to do a revision block (one page)

**Before you start (2 minutes)**
1. Pick the topic from the timetable. Get what you need: a pen, a blank page, flashcards or one past question. Phone out of the room.
2. Set a timer for 25 minutes. Write the topic at the top of your page.

**The 25 minutes: choose the method on the timetable**
- **Blank page (blurt):** Close the book. Write everything you can remember about the topic. Open the book, add what you missed in a different colour. The colour is your gap list.
- **Flashcards:** Question on the front, answer on the back. Say the answer out loud before turning the card. Cards you got wrong go back in the pile. Don't re-read cards you already know.
- **Past questions:** Try the question cold. Mark with the mark scheme. Write each mistake in your **error log**: the question, what went wrong, and the correct method.
- **Teach-back:** Explain the topic out loud as if teaching a younger sibling. Where you get stuck is where to revise.

**For maths:** Do a worked example, cover the solution, redo it yourself, then try fresh questions. Algebra and ratio only improve by doing questions, not by reading.

**For history dates:** Don't learn dates in isolation. Build a timeline, put events in a story ("this led to that"), and for each date write one sentence on why it mattered. Say dates out loud. Test the order, then the year.

**After (3 minutes)**
- 5-minute break: stand up, drink water, no phone scrolling.
- Write one line: what you did, and a confidence score out of 5 (red, amber or green).
- Tick the block on your timetable.

**If you miss a block:** don't try to double up. Use the catch-up slot (Sunday B, or Friday in Week 3).

## Weekly check-in (Sunday, 10 minutes, student with a parent or teacher)

1. **What got done?** Count the ticked blocks out of 11. Aim for about 9 or 10, not 11. That's a good week.
2. **What needs moving?** Missed blocks go into catch-up or next week. Drop something if the week was too much.
3. **Confidence per topic.** Colour each topic red, amber or green:

| Topic | Red / Amber / Green | Action for next week |
|---|---|---|
| Algebra | | |
| Ratio | | |
| History H1 | | |

4. **Error log review:** Look at the top 3 repeated mistakes. They become next week's maths fix-it blocks.
5. **One win and one thing to change.** Write one of each. Then switch off for the rest of Sunday.

## Notes for the teacher

- **Buffer:** one catch-up slot a week (about 9%) plus the freed Sunday afternoon. Don't fill it.
- **Why only 11 blocks:** a student who has never revised and loses focus after 30 minutes will follow a plan that's realistic. Add blocks only once they're hitting 9 or 10 a week.
- **Reducing history anxiety:** the three blurt checks per week on dates are the fastest win.
- **Student data:** no name or personal details are used in this plan. Add them yourself if your school's policy allows.
