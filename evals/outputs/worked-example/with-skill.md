# Solving Equations with the Unknown on Both Sides

**Year 9 practice sheet**

*Assumptions: one lesson of work, no calculators needed, and answers are whole numbers except where the twist question says otherwise. The sheet fades support in stages, so work through it in order. Teacher answers are at the end.*

---

## The method (same four steps every time)

| Step | What you do |
|---|---|
| **1** | Get the x terms on one side. Subtract the **smaller** x term from **both** sides. |
| **2** | Get the plain numbers on the other side. Add or subtract from **both** sides. |
| **3** | Divide both sides by the number in front of x. |
| **4** | Check: put your answer into the left side and the right side of the **original** equation. They must match. |

> **Watch out (Step 1):** this is where most mistakes happen. Whatever you do, you do to **both** sides. If you take away 2x from the left, you must take away 2x from the right too. Also, subtract the *smaller* x term so you don't end up with a negative number of x's.

---

## 1. Fully worked example

**Solve 5x + 3 = 2x + 15**

| Step | Working | Thinking |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | 5x + 3 = 2x + 15<br>−2x  −2x<br>**3x + 3 = 15** | The x terms are 5x and 2x. 2x is smaller, so I take 2x from **both** sides. Now x is only on the left. |
| **2** | 3x + 3 = 15<br>−3  −3<br>**3x = 12** | The +3 is in the way of the x term. The opposite of +3 is −3, so I subtract 3 from both sides. |
| **3** | 3x = 12<br>÷3  ÷3<br>**x = 4** | 3x means 3 × x. The opposite of multiplying by 3 is dividing by 3. |
| **4** | Left: 5(4) + 3 = 23<br>Right: 2(4) + 15 = 23<br>**23 = 23 ✓** | Both sides give the same number, so x = 4 works. |

---

## 2. Paired example (one thing changes)

**Solve 2x + 19 = 5x + 4**

*What's different: the bigger x term is now on the **right**. Everything else stays the same.*

| Step | Working | Thinking |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | 2x + 19 = 5x + 4<br>−2x  −2x<br>**19 = 3x + 4** | The smaller x term is 2x (on the left), so I still subtract 2x from both sides. This time the x ends up on the right, and that's fine. |
| **2** | 19 = 3x + 4<br>−4  −4<br>**15 = 3x** | Same idea: subtract 4 from both sides to leave 3x on its own. |
| **3** | 15 = 3x<br>÷3  ÷3<br>**5 = x** | Divide both sides by 3. "5 = x" means the same as "x = 5". |
| **4** | Left: 2(5) + 19 = 29<br>Right: 5(5) + 4 = 29<br>**29 = 29 ✓** | Both sides match. |

**What changed and what didn't:** the x ended up on the other side, but the four steps were identical.

## 3. Faded example 1: you do Step 4

**Solve 7x + 2 = 3x + 30**

| Step | Working | Thinking |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | 7x + 2 = 3x + 30<br>−3x  −3x<br>**4x + 2 = 30** | Subtract the smaller x term (3x) from both sides. |
| **2** | 4x + 2 = 30<br>−2  −2<br>**4x = 28** | Subtract 2 from both sides. |
| **3** | 4x = 28<br>÷4  ÷4<br>**x = 7** | Divide both sides by 4. |
| **4** | Left: ____________<br>Right: ____________<br>____ = ____ ✓ | **Your turn.** Check x = 7 in the original equation. |

## 4. Faded example 2: you do Steps 2, 3 and 4

**Solve 9x − 2 = 4x + 13**

| Step | Working | Thinking |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | 9x − 2 = 4x + 13<br>−4x  −4x<br>**5x − 2 = 13** | Subtract the smaller x term (4x) from both sides. |
| **2** | 5x − 2 = 13<br>____  ____<br>5x = ____ | **Your turn.** The −2 is in the way. What is the opposite of −2? |
| **3** | ____ | **Your turn.** Divide both sides by the number in front of x. |
| **4** | Left: ____________<br>Right: ____________ | **Your turn.** Check in the original equation. |

> **Careful:** the opposite of "−2" is "+2". Adding 2 to both sides is what leaves 5x on its own.

## 6. Independent problems

Use all four steps, and always do Step 4. They get harder as you go.

1. 6x + 4 = 4x + 20

# Answers (teacher copy)

**Faded example 1:** Step 4: Left 7(7) + 2 = 51; Right 3(7) + 30 = 51. ✓

**Faded example 2:**
- Step 2: add 2 to both sides: 5x = 15
- Step 3: x = 3
- Step 4: Left 9(3) − 2 = 25; Right 4(3) + 13 = 25 ✓

**Faded example 3:**
- Step 2: add 14 to both sides: 24 = 4x
- Step 3: 6 = x, so x = 6
- Step 4: Left 3(6) + 10 = 28; Right 7(6) − 14 = 28 ✓

**Independent problems**

| # | Step 1 | Step 2 | Step 3 | Check |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2x + 4 = 20 | 2x = 16 | **x = 8** | 52 = 52 ✓ |
| 2 | 3x + 3 = 30 | 3x = 27 | **x = 9** | 75 = 75 ✓ |
| 3 | 21 = 5x + 1 | 20 = 5x | **x = 4** | 29 = 29 ✓ |
| 4 | 6x − 7 = 23 | 6x = 30 | **x = 5** | 43 = 43 ✓ |
| 5 | 5 = 4x + 21 | −16 = 4x | **x = −4** | −7 = −7 ✓ |

**Common errors to watch for**
- Subtracting the x term from one side only (Step 1).
- Subtracting the *larger* x term, giving a negative x coefficient. This can still work but is much harder to handle, so steer students to the smaller term.
- Subtracting 2 instead of adding 2 when the equation has "− 2" (Faded 2, Faded 3, Question 4).
- In Question 5, stopping at "−16 = 4x" or dropping the minus sign when dividing.
