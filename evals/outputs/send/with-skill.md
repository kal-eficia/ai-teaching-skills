# Adapting the Year 8 Civil War extended answer

**Task:** "Charles I was mainly to blame for the outbreak of the Civil War." How far do you agree? (Three sources, pp. 42-44, 30 minutes, PEEL, judgement.)

## Assumptions

- I haven't seen your students' support plans, so I've worked from the three needs you named. Check the plans or SENCO advice for anything specific, such as a reader or scribe arrangement or extra time. I haven't diagnosed anything.
- Most of this helps the whole class, so I've written it as a resource for everyone with extra layers for the three students.
- Please refer to the students by initials or numbers if you share the adapted sheet or anything about them, and keep diagnoses off anything that goes on display.

## The principle

Same question, same sources, same expectation of a judgement. What changes is the route: the layout, the chunking, the predictability and the scaffolds. Nothing below lowers the destination.

## 1. Adapted worksheet (use for the whole class, with the extras below for the three students)

### Today's task

**The big question:** "Charles I was mainly to blame for the outbreak of the Civil War." How far do you agree?

**What you will do:** write 3 paragraphs and a short conclusion, using the 3 sources and what you already know.
**Time:** 30 minutes. The timings are below.
**You are finished when:** all 5 boxes on your checklist are ticked.

### Key words (read these first)

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Blame** | Who or what is responsible for something going wrong |
| **Mainly** | The biggest part, but not the only part |
| **How far do you agree?** | Give your opinion, with reasons, and say how strong your agreement is |
| **Judgement** | Your final decision on the question |
| **Source** | A document, picture or account from the past |

### Paragraph plan (choose your three from the list or use your own)

Possible factors: Charles's actions and beliefs, Parliament's demands and behaviour, religion, money and taxes, Scotland and Ireland, the personalities involved.

Write three paragraphs. Use **at least one** source in two of them.

**Paragraph box (copy this three times)**

1. **P (Point):** One factor that caused the war is ______________.
2. **E (Evidence):** Source ___ says/shows ______________. Or from my own knowledge: ______________.
3. **E (Explain):** This shows that ______________ because ______________.
4. **L (Link):** So this means Charles I was **more / less / equally** to blame because ______________.

### Conclusion starters

- "Overall, I agree / partly agree / disagree that Charles I was mainly to blame because ______________."
- "The most important cause was ______________ because ______________."
- "Charles's actions mattered, but ______________ also mattered because ______________."

### Layout notes for you (the teacher) when you print

- Sans-serif font (Arial or similar), 12 to 14 pt, 1.5 line spacing, left-aligned, not justified.
- Cream or pale pastel paper if the student finds white glare hard to read. Ask them, don't assume.
- Number every instruction and keep to one step per line.
- Leave real writing space in the boxes.

## 2. Extra support for each student

### Student with dyslexia

- **Reading:** give the three sources in large print with a line of space between paragraphs, or as audio, or have them read aloud in advance. Let the student highlight the key phrases rather than reading them cold under time pressure.
- **Glossary:** pre-teach or pre-define any hard source vocabulary on a side box (for example "absolute monarch", "Parliament", "Divine Right", "ship money"). Spelling should not cost marks for key terms, so put a word bank on the sheet.
- **Writing:** allow typing, a scribe or a voice recorder if that is their normal way of working. Don't make them copy the question from the board.
- **Marking:** mark for the history (judgement, use of evidence, explanation), not spelling and handwriting. Say this to the student before they start.
- **Time:** check whether the plan allows extra time. If not, reduce what is required (see "fewer, bigger" below) rather than expect speed.

### Student with ADHD

- **Chunking:** the 5-step plan above is the main support. Give a visible clock or sand timer for each step, and have them tick each box as they finish.
- **Fewer, bigger tasks:** ask for **two** PEEL paragraphs plus a conclusion, not three, if the full set is too much. The judgement still has to happen, so the conclusion stays. This keeps the demand on thinking, not on volume.
- **Clear start and end:** give a "start here" arrow on the sheet and tell them exactly what "done" looks like (the checklist).
- **Movement and roles:** let them collect the sources or the resources, or hand out the sheets, before the task begins. A short stand-and-stretch break between step 2 and step 3 helps. Seat them away from the busiest part of the room if possible.
- **Check-ins:** a quiet check at the end of steps 2 and 3, a thumbs-up or a short "what's your first point?" is enough.

### Autistic student who gets anxious about open-ended tasks

The anxiety is mainly about not knowing what "right" looks like. Make the task less open without making it easier.

- **Show the structure early:** before the lesson, or at the start, give a written one-page lesson plan: 5 min reading, 5 min grid, 15 min writing, 4 min conclusion, 1 min check. Warn before each change ("2 minutes left on step 2").
- **Literal instructions:** replace or explain phrases that could be read oddly. "How far do you agree?" should be explained as: "Say whether you agree, disagree or partly agree, and give reasons." "Reach a judgement" means "make a final decision and say why." "Using your own knowledge" means "add facts you have learned in lessons."
- **Narrow the choice:** instead of "choose any factors", give a short menu of three suggested paragraph topics (for example Charles's actions, money and taxes, Parliament). Offer their own choice as an option, not a requirement. This still demands the same thinking, but the starting point is clear.
- **A model:** a short worked example of one PEEL paragraph on a different question (for example "Why did the Gunpowder Plot fail?") shows what a finished paragraph looks like.
- **Success criteria:** list exactly what a strong answer includes: a clear view on the question, at least 3 reasons, evidence from sources, own knowledge, and a conclusion that says which reason was biggest.
- **Working alone:** if there is any pair or discussion before writing, offer the option to work alone and to have a defined role if they prefer pair work.
- **An agreed signal:** a card or phrase they can use if they feel overwhelmed, with a known calm option (for example, a few minutes at the back of the room). Agree it before the lesson.

## 3. Whole-class sentence-level supports (so no one is singled out)

Put these in the lesson for everyone: the key words box, the evidence grid, the PEEL boxes and the conclusion starters. The three students use the same sheet as their peers, with their individual extras added quietly.

## 4. Note on what changed and why

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| Numbered steps with timings and a checklist | Chunks a 30-minute open task so it is manageable for ADHD, and predictable for the autistic student |
| Key words box and plain-English instruction | Removes ambiguity and reading load (dyslexia, autism) |
| Evidence grid before writing | Separates reading and notes from writing, so the student isn't doing everything at once |
| PEEL boxes with sentence starters | Gives a structure to hold the thinking, which cuts anxiety about "where to start" |
| Conclusion starters | Makes the judgement achievable without telling them what to think |
| Menu of suggested factors | Reduces the open-endedness without removing the decision |
| Typing, scribe or audio options | Lets the student show historical thinking without being blocked by spelling or handwriting |

**What did not change:** the question, the sources, the need to use evidence and own knowledge, and the need for a reasoned judgement.

## 5. When to fade each support

| Support | When to reduce it |
|---|---|
| Key words box | After two or three tasks once the terms are secure, move it to a separate knowledge organiser. |
| Evidence grid | After the student completes it independently twice, switch to a blank margin-notes version. |
| PEEL sentence starters | Remove one starter at a time, first the Point, then the Explain. Keep the Link until last. |
| Conclusion starters | Replace with a single prompt ("Say which factor mattered most and why") after two or three attempts. |
| Menu of factors | Widen to "choose any three" once the student has done it successfully with a menu. |
| Timed step plan | Keep it for the whole year for ADHD and autism if it helps. For other students, turn it into a single timing line. |
| Worked example | Keep it available. Remove it from the sheet once the student can write a paragraph independently. |
| Typed or scribed answers | Do not fade if it is an agreed access arrangement. Review with the SENCO. |

Review after two or three lessons with the students themselves: ask what helped, what you could drop, and what you should add. Keep what works.
