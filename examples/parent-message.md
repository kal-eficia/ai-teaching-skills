# Example: parent-message

## The request

> Use parent-message. Two WhatsApp messages. 1) To [S2]'s mum: great progress this term, and I'd like [S2] to keep using the revision app 10 minutes three evenings a week over the holiday. 2) To [S3]'s dad: he's missed 4 of 10 homeworks; I want him to start using the homework checklist I send on Mondays. Placeholders for names.

## What the skill produced

**1. Progress update (58 words)**

> Assalamu alaykum [parent],
>
> Quick good news: [S2] has gone from 41% to 68% this term, and it's down to her hard work.
>
> Over the holiday, [S2] will do 10 minutes on the revision app on Monday, Wednesday and Friday evenings. All you need to do is ask her what score she got.
>
> Thank you for your support,
> [Teacher]

**2. Concern (70 words)**

> Assalamu alaykum [parent],
>
> [S3] is one of the strongest programmers in his group, and I want his theory to catch up. He's missed 4 of the last 10 homeworks, and that's where the theory practice happens.
>
> From this Monday, [S3] will get a short homework checklist from me. Could you ask him on Monday evenings to show you it's ticked? Reply OK if that works for you.
>
> Thank you,
> [Teacher]

## Checked

- [x] No internal programme names: "the revision app", "a homework checklist".
- [x] One action each, and it's clear who does it: the student does the work, and the parent asks or checks.
- [x] Under 80 words, readable on a phone.
- [x] The concern opens with a real strength, then gives the fact and the plan. It doesn't list faults.
