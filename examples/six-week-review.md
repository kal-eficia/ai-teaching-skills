# Example: six-week-review

## The request

> Use six-week-review for [S2], IGCSE CS. Baseline diagnostic in week 1, same-style topic quizzes since. Attendance 12/12, homework 6/6. Topic 3 hardware has only just started, so it wasn't in the baseline.

## What the skill produced

### Baseline vs now (same question types both times)

| Topic | Week 1 | Week 7 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Binary and hex conversions | 50% | 85% | +35 |
| Two's complement | 20% | 45% | +25 |
| File size calculations | 40% | 75% | +35 |
| Compression | 60% | 80% | +20 |
| Packets and packet switching | 30% | 70% | +40 |
| Error checking (parity, checksum, echo, ARQ) | 25% | 50% | +25 |
| Encryption | 35% | 75% | +40 |
| **Overall** | **41%** | **68%** | **+27** |

*Hardware isn't compared: it wasn't in the baseline. It joins the next review.*

### What the numbers say

- **Working:** conversions, file sizes, packets and encryption all moved by 35 to 40 points. These were retrieved most often in starters, and it shows.
- **Moved, but not secure:** two's complement and error checking both gained 25 points but are still at 50% or below. Both are multi-step procedures where she loses a step (adding 1; parity counts).
- **Habits:** full attendance and every homework in. Confidence ratings are rising, and she's rarely confident and wrong, so her gaps are honest gaps.

### Next six weeks: three priorities

1. **Two's complement:** a worked-example sheet (see `worked-example-builder`), then one conversion in every starter for three weeks.
2. **Error checking:** a comparison table of the four methods, then two exam-style "explain" questions a week.
3. **Hardware:** baseline quiz on Topic 3 now, so the next review can compare it.

### Summary for [S2] and home

> [S2] has made one of the strongest starts in the group: up from 41% to 68% in six weeks, with every lesson attended and every homework done. The two areas to firm up are two's complement and error checking, both multi-step methods where one missed step costs the marks. [S2] will get a step-by-step sheet and a little practice in each lesson. At home, ask her to explain one method to you each week. Her steady effort is clearly paying off.
