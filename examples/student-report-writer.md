# Example: student-report-writer

## The request

> Use student-report-writer. End-of-term reports, IGCSE Computer Science, 60 to 90 words each. Placeholders instead of names; I'll swap them in.
>
> - **[S1]** (he): quizzes average 92%. Explains ideas really well out loud. Rushes 6-mark "explain" questions, usually gets 3 out of 6.
> - **[S2]** (she): baseline 41%, now 68%. Every homework in. Quiet on the mic but her chat answers are strong. Binary conversions still slip.
> - **[S3]** (he): 55%. Best Python programmer in the group. Missed 4 of 10 homeworks. Fetch-decode-execute still muddled.
> - **[S4]** (she): 74%. Confident, helps others in the chat. Confident but wrong on storage topics; doesn't check her work.

## What the skill produced

| Student | Report | Words |
|---|---|---|
| [S1] | [S1] can explain how a CPU works better than almost anyone in the group. When he talks a concept through, it's clear, accurate and confident, and his quiz average of 92% reflects that. The step up now is getting that same depth onto paper. On 6-mark questions he often stops at three points, so next term he'll plan each one with six quick bullet points before writing. With that habit, he's ready for top grades. | 75 |
| [S2] | This term [S2] has gone from 41% to 68%, one of the biggest improvements in the class, and it's come from steady work: every homework, on time. In live lessons her written answers in the chat are often the most precise. Binary conversions are where marks still slip, so ten minutes of conversion practice twice a week will lock them in. Her consistency is her strength, and it's working. | 69 |
| [S3] | [S3] is a natural programmer. In practical lessons his Python solutions are often the neatest in the group, and he enjoys finding a better way to do things. Theory needs the same energy: the fetch-decode-execute cycle isn't secure yet, and 4 of 10 homeworks were missed, which is where that practice happens. If he completes every homework next term, his theory will catch up with his coding, and that combination is powerful. | 72 |
| [S4] | [S4] brings real energy to lessons and often helps classmates in the chat, which shows how well she understands much of the course. Her 74% average is solid. The next step is checking her answers before submitting, especially on storage, where she's sometimes certain but wrong. Reading each answer back against the question will turn those near-misses into marks. She has everything she needs to push into the top band. | 70 |

**Repeat check:** no sentence or opening is shared across the four reports. Openings: a skill, a measured improvement, a practical strength, a classroom habit.

**Flagged:** none. Each student had at least one specific piece of evidence.

## Why it's useful

- Each report is a sandwich (strength, one doable next step, a positive close), and each next step names a specific habit, not a character trait.
- No jargon parents would need explained ("AO2", "working towards").
- Names never went into the AI tool.
